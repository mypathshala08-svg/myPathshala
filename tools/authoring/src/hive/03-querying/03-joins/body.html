1 The story

“Why are Chennai and Navi Mumbai missing from the target report?”

Before you start: you need GROUP BY (Lesson 3.2). The small city_targets and price_bands tables are created in “Run on your PC”.

Kabir joins TiffinGo’s revenue per city with the targets table to show achievement. The report has six cities. The CEO asks where Chennai and Navi Mumbai went, and why Nashik, the newest city with a target but no orders yet, is not there either.

K> Sir, the join worked. It just… lost three cities. A> An inner join keeps only rows that match on both sides. Chennai has revenue but no target row; Nashik has a target but no orders. Both are dropped. The question decides the join: “all cities with revenue” is a left join, “every city on either list” is a full outer join, “targets with no orders” is an anti join. R> And for big tables? Joining 50,000 orders with seven targets? A> Hive sees that the targets table is tiny and broadcasts it to every map task: a map join, no shuffle at all. We will read that in EXPLAIN, and meet the classic traps: a WHERE filter that silently turns a left join into an inner join, and NULL keys that never match.
2 Definition

Joins in HiveQL

Formal definition (write this in exams)

A join combines rows of two or more tables according to a join condition in the ON clause. An inner join returns pairs of rows that satisfy the condition; a left/right/full outer join additionally returns unmatched rows of the left/right/both tables with NULLs for the other side; a left semi join returns each left row that has at least one match, without right-side columns; a cross join returns every combination. Hive executes joins as shuffle joins (both inputs partitioned by key and merged in reducers) or map joins (small tables broadcast to map tasks), chosen by the optimiser.

In simple words

A join puts rows from two tables side by side when their keys match. The join type decides what happens to rows without a partner: dropped (inner), kept with NULLs (outer), or used only as a yes/no test (semi, anti). Hive either shuffles both tables by key or copies a small table to every task.

Words you’ll meet in this lesson

Join keyThe column(s) compared in ON, e.g. city or customer_id.
Outer sideThe table whose unmatched rows are kept (left in a LEFT JOIN).
Semi joinKeep left rows that have a match; return only left columns, no duplicates from the right.
Anti joinKeep left rows that have no match. Written with NOT EXISTS in Hive.

Map joinSmall table broadcast to all map tasks; no shuffle of the big table.

LEVELS> Joins at three levels L1> Matching two class registers
  • You have a list of students who paid fees and a list of students who sat the exam. A join lines up rows that share a key (the roll number).
  • Inner: only students on both lists. Left: everyone who paid, with exam details where they exist. Full: everyone on either list.
  • Semi: “who paid and also sat the exam?” (only the first list’s columns). Anti: “who paid but did not sit?”.
  • Missing details on an outer side are shown as NULL.
L2> Syntax and rules in Hive
  • [INNER] JOIN, LEFT|RIGHT|FULL [OUTER] JOIN, LEFT SEMI JOIN, CROSS JOIN, all with ON.
  • Anti join: Hive 4.2.1 rejected LEFT ANTI JOIN syntax in our lab; write NOT EXISTS (…) or LEFT JOIN … WHERE right.key IS NULL.
  • LEFT SEMI JOIN: the right table may appear only in ON; returns each left row at most once.
  • Joins are evaluated before WHERE. A WHERE condition on the right table of a LEFT JOIN removes the NULL rows and behaves like an inner join; put such conditions in ON.
  • NULL keys never match with =; use <=> if NULL should match NULL.
  • Non-equality conditions (e.g. BETWEEN bands) are allowed in ON since Hive 2.2.
L3> How Hive executes a join
  • Shuffle (common) join: both tables are repartitioned by the join key and matched in reducers (Merge Join Operator, two SIMPLE_EDGEs).
  • Map (broadcast) join: the small table is loaded into memory in every map task; the big one is streamed with no shuffle (Map Join Operator, BROADCAST_EDGE). Chosen automatically by hive.auto.convert.join using size estimates (threshold hive.auto.convert.join.noconditionaltask.size).
  • Several joins on the same key can run in one stage; different keys need separate shuffles.
  • In a shuffle join, the table streamed through the reducer is the last one (or the one named with the STREAMTABLE hint); others are buffered.
  • Bucket map joins and sort-merge-bucket joins need bucketed tables (Module 8).
  • Joins are not commutative for outer joins and are evaluated left to right.

4 Step by step

Revenue vs target, every way

All outputs are real (Hive 4.2.1 lab). s is a CTE with revenue per city: WITH s AS (SELECT city, SUM(amount) AS revenue FROM orders GROUP BY city).

  1. Step 1 · Inner join: only matches

    WITH s AS (SELECT city, SUM(amount) AS revenue FROM orders GROUP BY city)
SELECT s.city, s.revenue, t.monthly_target
FROM s JOIN city_targets t ON s.city = t.city ORDER BY s.city;
    +------------+-------------+-------------------+ | s.city | s.revenue | t.monthly_target | +------------+-------------+-------------------+ | Bengaluru | 2724070.00 | 2600000.00 | | Delhi | 1614230.00 | 1600000.00 | | Hyderabad | 1763190.00 | 1700000.00 | | Mumbai | 2941120.00 | 2800000.00 | | Pune | 2344660.00 | 2000000.00 | | Thane | 1408910.00 | 1300000.00 | +------------+-------------+-------------------+
    Six rows. Chennai and Navi Mumbai (no target) and Nashik (no orders) are dropped without any warning: exactly the CEO’s question.

  2. Step 2 · Left join: keep every revenue city

    … FROM s LEFT JOIN city_targets t ON s.city = t.city ORDER BY s.city;
    +--------------+-------------+-------------------+ | s.city | s.revenue | t.monthly_target | +--------------+-------------+-------------------+ | Bengaluru | 2724070.00 | 2600000.00 | | Chennai | 752250.00 | NULL | | Delhi | 1614230.00 | 1600000.00 | | Hyderabad | 1763190.00 | 1700000.00 | | Mumbai | 2941120.00 | 2800000.00 | | Navi Mumbai | 1172750.00 | NULL | | Pune | 2344660.00 | 2000000.00 | | Thane | 1408910.00 | 1300000.00 | +--------------+-------------+-------------------+
    All 8 revenue cities; the two without a target show NULL. This is the right shape for “achievement per city”, with NULL meaning “no target set”.

  3. Step 3 · Full outer join: everyone

    SELECT s.city AS s_city, t.city AS t_city, s.revenue, t.monthly_target
FROM s FULL OUTER JOIN city_targets t ON s.city = t.city
ORDER BY COALESCE(s.city, t.city);
    +--------------+------------+-------------+-------------------+ | s_city | t_city | s.revenue | t.monthly_target | +--------------+------------+-------------+-------------------+ | Bengaluru | Bengaluru | 2724070.00 | 2600000.00 | | Chennai | NULL | 752250.00 | NULL | | Delhi | Delhi | 1614230.00 | 1600000.00 | | Hyderabad | Hyderabad | 1763190.00 | 1700000.00 | | Mumbai | Mumbai | 2941120.00 | 2800000.00 | | NULL | Nashik | NULL | 500000.00 | | Navi Mumbai | NULL | 1172750.00 | NULL | | Pune | Pune | 2344660.00 | 2000000.00 | | Thane | Thane | 1408910.00 | 1300000.00 | +--------------+------------+-------------+-------------------+
    Nine cities: six matched, two with only revenue, one (Nashik) with only a target. COALESCE(s.city, t.city) gives one city name for sorting and display.

  4. Step 4 · Semi and anti joins

    SELECT t.city FROM city_targets t LEFT SEMI JOIN orders o ON t.city = o.city;
SELECT t.city FROM city_targets t LEFT ANTI JOIN orders o ON t.city = o.city;
SELECT t.city FROM city_targets t
WHERE NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM orders o WHERE o.city = t.city);
    6 rows: Bengaluru, Delhi, Hyderabad, Mumbai, Pune, Thane Error: Error while compiling statement: FAILED: ParseException line 1:39 cannot recognize input near 'LEFT' 'ANTI' 'JOIN' in join type specifier +---------+ | t.city | +---------+ | Nashik | +---------+
    The semi join returns each target city once, even though Pune has 7,930 matching orders. Hive 4.2.1 does not accept LEFT ANTI JOIN as syntax, but NOT EXISTS (or LEFT JOIN … WHERE o.city IS NULL) finds Nashik.

  5. Step 5 · The WHERE trap in outer joins

    -- filter in WHERE
… FROM s LEFT JOIN city_targets t ON s.city = t.city WHERE t.region = 'West';
-- filter in ON
… FROM s LEFT JOIN city_targets t ON s.city = t.city AND t.region = 'West';
    +---------+-----------+ | s.city | t.region | +---------+-----------+ | Mumbai | West | | Pune | West | | Thane | West | +---------+-----------+ +--------------+-----------+ | s.city | t.region | +--------------+-----------+ | Bengaluru | NULL | | Chennai | NULL | | Delhi | NULL | | Hyderabad | NULL | | Mumbai | West | | Navi Mumbai | NULL | | Pune | West | | Thane | West | +--------------+-----------+
    In WHERE, the condition runs after the join and drops rows whose region is NULL: 3 rows, an inner join in disguise. In ON, it only decides which target rows match: all 8 cities stay, non-West ones with NULL.

  6. Step 6 · NULL keys and the NULL-safe join

    -- a: {NULL, 'x'}   b: {NULL, 'x'}
SELECT a.k, b.k FROM a JOIN b ON a.k = b.k;
SELECT a.k, b.k FROM a JOIN b ON a.k <=> b.k;
    +------+------+ | a_k | b_k | +------+------+ | x | x | +------+------+ +-------+-------+ | a_k | b_k | +-------+-------+ | x | x | | NULL | NULL | +-------+-------+
    With = the NULL keys never match (NULL = NULL is NULL). With <=> they do. Decide on purpose: unknown customer ids usually should not join to each other.

  7. Step 7 · Map join or shuffle join? Read EXPLAIN

    EXPLAIN SELECT o.order_id, t.region FROM orders o JOIN city_targets t ON o.city = t.city;
SET hive.auto.convert.join=false;   -- force a shuffle join, for comparison
EXPLAIN SELECT o.order_id, t.region FROM orders o JOIN city_targets t ON o.city = t.city;
    -- default: Map 1 <- Map 2 (BROADCAST_EDGE) Map 1 vectorized Map Join Operator [MAPJOIN_22] (rows=43750 width=92) <-Map 2 [BROADCAST_EDGE] vectorized -- auto.convert.join=false: Reducer 2 <- Map 1 (SIMPLE_EDGE), Map 3 (SIMPLE_EDGE) Reducer 2 Merge Join Operator [MERGEJOIN_15] (rows=43750 width=92) <-Map 1 [SIMPLE_EDGE] vectorized <-Map 3 [SIMPLE_EDGE] vectorized (lines picked from the plans)
    By default the 7-row targets table is broadcast (BROADCAST_EDGE) and the join happens inside the map task reading orders: no reducer. Switched off, both tables are shuffled to Reducer 2 (SIMPLE_EDGE twice). Same result, very different cost on big data.

Which rows survive each join

Four cities, six joins 6 steps
What you see
  1. The inner join returns six cities.
  2. The left join keeps all eight revenue cities; two have no target.
  3. The full outer join returns nine cities, with NULLs on both sides.
  4. The semi join finds six target cities with orders; NOT EXISTS finds Nashik.
  5. The same filter in WHERE returns 3 rows, in ON returns 8.
  6. EXPLAIN shows a broadcast map join by default and a shuffle join when automatic conversion is off.
Behind the screen
  1. Only rows with a partner on both sides are paired.
  2. Unmatched left rows are kept and the right columns are filled with NULL.
  3. Unmatched rows from both sides are kept and padded with NULL.
  4. Semi and anti joins only test whether a partner exists; they add no columns.
  5. The join runs first; WHERE then removes rows where region is NULL, undoing the LEFT join.
  6. The tiny table is copied into memory of every map task, so the big table never moves across the network.

Three live scenarios follow: duplicate keys that explode a join, a star-schema report and a skewed shuffle join.

Real-time scenario 1

The join that doubled revenue

Duplicate keys are the most common join bug in reporting. (Numbers illustrative.)

Join explosion 5 steps
Animated example
  1. A report joins orders to customer addresses and revenue comes out far too high.
  2. Customers with several addresses multiply their orders: 5 orders × 3 addresses = 15 rows.
  3. Check key uniqueness with GROUP BY … HAVING COUNT(*) > 1 before joining.
  4. Join to exactly one address per customer, or aggregate the right side first.
  5. Compare row counts before and after the join.
Real-time scenario 2

A star schema report: one fact, several dimensions

Warehouse reports join a big fact table to small dimension tables. The numbers here are real lab results.

Star joins 5 steps
Animated example
  1. Orders is the fact table; customers, targets, restaurants and price bands are dimensions.
  2. One query joins orders to customers and targets and groups by plan and region.
  3. Small dimensions are broadcast, so the fact table is read once without shuffling for the joins.
  4. A non-equi join assigns each order to a price band.
  5. Filter early, keep statistics fresh and let the optimiser order the joins.
Real-time scenario 3 · advanced

A shuffle join stuck on one key

Skew hurts joins as much as GROUP BY. Two common causes and their fixes. (Numbers illustrative.)

Skewed joins 5 steps
Animated example
  1. A shuffle join stalls: one reducer receives a third of all rows.
  2. They are NULL keys, which can never match; filter them out before the join.
  3. For a genuine hot key, Hive’s skew-join settings process it separately.
  4. Salting spreads a hot key over several reducers.
  5. Diagnose with task times and key counts before changing settings.
6 Real-world use

Joins in real pipelines

1. Enriching facts with dimensions

Order facts get customer plan, restaurant cuisine and city region from dimension tables before reports group by them.

Facts→JOIN dimensions→Enriched table

2. Targets vs actuals

Business reviews compare actual revenue with targets per city or team. A full outer join shows both missing targets and missing sales.

Actuals→FULL OUTER JOIN→Variance report

3. Finding gaps

Customers who signed up but never ordered, payments with no matching order, orders with no delivery record: anti joins find the gaps reconciliation teams chase.

Two systems→Anti join→Exceptions list

4. Range lookups

Tax slabs, delivery-fee bands and loyalty tiers live in small range tables; a non-equi join assigns each transaction to its slab.

Transactions→ON x BETWEEN lo AND hi→Slab per row

5. Join quality checks

Pipelines compare row counts before and after each join and test key uniqueness, so duplicated or lost rows stop the job instead of reaching dashboards.

Join→Count checks→Publish or stop
