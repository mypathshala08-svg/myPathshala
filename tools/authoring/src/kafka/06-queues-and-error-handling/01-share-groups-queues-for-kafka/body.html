1 The story

More workers than partitions

Before you start: 3.1 Consumer groups.

🤔 A topic with 2 partitions, and you want 10 chefs working on it at once. A normal consumer group says “only 2”. What now?

Kafka 4.x has share groups (KIP-932, “Queues for Kafka”): consumers share partitions record by record, like a job queue. Each record is locked to one consumer, then acknowledged, released for another try, or rejected. In our lab, 3,000 jobs were processed with zero duplicates, and a released record came back exactly 5 times before Kafka gave up on it.

2 Definition

Share groups (Queues for Kafka)

Formal definition (write this in exams)

A share group (KIP-932) is a set of consumers that cooperatively consume records from topics without exclusive partition assignment. The broker delivers records in batches under an acquisition lock; each record is acknowledged by the consumer as accepted, released or rejected. The broker tracks per-record delivery counts and a share-partition start offset, persisted by the share coordinator, giving at-least-once, unordered, queue-like consumption.

In simple words

A share group lets many consumers pull jobs from the same partitions. Each job is locked to one worker until it says done, try again, or give up. Like a queue, but built into Kafka.

Why, how, where

Why

Consumer groups cap parallelism at the partition count and block a partition on one slow record. Work queues (emails, image jobs, tickets) want “any free worker takes the next job”.

the problem it solves

How

Consumers join a share group (group.type share). The broker hands out batches of records with a time-limited lock; the consumer acknowledges each record: ACCEPT (done), RELEASE (give to someone again), REJECT (unprocessable). Unacknowledged records return when the lock expires. Each delivery increments a count; at the limit the record is archived.

the mechanism

Where

Job queues, notification senders, ML inference workers, anything where order does not matter but throughput and elastic workers do. Previously teams used RabbitMQ/SQS next to Kafka for this.

in real systems

When not

No ordering guarantee across records, even in one partition. Offsets are tracked by the broker (share-partition), not committed by you. Needs a recent Kafka (early access in 4.0, preview in 4.1, generally available in 4.2; share.version ≥ 1) and a client that supports it (Java KafkaShareConsumer; other clients are catching up).

Life of one record

LEVELS> Share groups at three levels L1> Token counter at a canteen
  • Orders come on a counter. Any free cook picks the next ticket, writes their name on it (lock), and stamps it DONE when served. If a cook leaves without stamping, after a while the ticket goes back. A ticket that fails 5 times goes to the manager’s file.
L2> The mechanics
  • Create by using a share consumer with a group id: the group is a share group (separate from consumer groups; listed by kafka-share-groups.sh).
  • Record states: Available → Acquired (locked, share.record.lock.duration.ms 30 s) → Acknowledged or back to Available (release/timeout) or Archived (reject or share.delivery.count.limit 5 reached).
  • Implicit mode: everything returned by poll() is accepted on the next poll. Explicit mode (share.acknowledgement.mode=explicit): call acknowledge(record, ACCEPT|RELEASE|REJECT) then commitSync().
L3> Under the hood
  • Each share-partition keeps a Share-Partition Start Offset (SPSO) and an in-flight window up to share.partition.max.record.locks (2000) records. Everything below SPSO is done.
  • State is persisted by the share coordinator in the internal topic __share_group_state, so it survives broker failover.
  • Where a share group starts reading is share.auto.offset.reset (latest by default; we set earliest with kafka-configs on the group). Ordering is not guaranteed, and a retried record may be processed after newer ones.
/LEVELS

Myth vs reality: things many websites still say

  • Myth: Kafka cannot be a queue.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): Share groups (4.x) are queues in Kafka.
    Proof: Step 2.
  • Myth: More consumers than partitions sit idle.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): Not in a share group.
    Proof: Scenario 1.
  • Myth: A failing record retries forever.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): Archived at delivery count 5.
    Proof: Step 5: 60 = 12×5.
EXAM> “Share group vs consumer group?” Share group: records (not partitions) distributed, per-record locks and acks (accept/release/reject), delivery count limit, no ordering, consumers can exceed partitions. Consumer group: exclusive partitions, ordered, committed offsets.

4 Step by step

Share, reject, release

All outputs are real: Kafka 4.4.0 (share.version 2), 3 brokers, console share consumer.

  1. Step 1 · A topic with only 2 partitions

    kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server k1:9092 --create --topic jobs-61 --partitions 2 --replication-factor 3
kafka-configs.sh --bootstrap-server k1:9092 --entity-type groups --entity-name kitchen-61 --alter --add-config share.auto.offset.reset=earliest
seq 1 3000 | sed "s/^/job-/" | kafka-console-producer.sh --bootstrap-server k1:9092 --topic jobs-61
    == 1) topic with 2 partitions, 3000 cooking jobs (we show counts) Partition:0 leader 1 Partition:1 leader 2
    The group config makes the new share group start from the beginning.

  2. Step 2 · Three share consumers

    for c in A B C; do kafka-console-share-consumer.sh --bootstrap-server k1:9092 --topic jobs-61 --group kitchen-61 --timeout-ms 10000 > chef_$c.txt & done; wait
    == 2) three share consumers in one share group (3 consumers > 2 partitions) chef A got job-1 chef A got job-2 chef B got job-2118 chef B got job-2119 chef A total: 2117 chef B total: 883 chef C total: 0 all: 3000 records, unique: 3000
    Two chefs shared the work and 3,000 records were delivered with 3,000 unique: no duplicates. Chef C got nothing: the console consumer fetches big batches, and A and B had locked everything before C joined. With slower, real processing (or smaller max.poll.records), work spreads across all members.

  3. Step 3 · Describe

    kafka-share-groups.sh --bootstrap-server k1:9092 --describe --group kitchen-61
    == 3) describe the share group GROUP TOPIC PARTITION START-OFFSET LAG kitchen-61 jobs-61 0 0 0 kitchen-61 jobs-61 1 1093 0
    START-OFFSET is the SPSO per partition and LAG 0 means nothing left. There is no “current offset per consumer” as in a consumer group.

  4. Step 4 · Reject

    kafka-console-share-consumer.sh … --topic small-61 --group rej2-61 --reject
kafka-console-share-consumer.sh … --topic small-61 --group rej2-61
    == 4) --reject: records are archived, never redelivered first pass rejected: 12 second pass got: 0
    Rejected records are archived immediately; a second pass gets nothing.

  5. Step 5 · Release until the limit

    kafka-configs.sh … --entity-type groups --entity-name rel2-61 --describe --all | grep -E "delivery|lock"
kafka-console-share-consumer.sh … --topic small-61 --group rel2-61 --release   # 3 runs
    == 5) --release: records come back; after the delivery-count limit they are archived share.delivery.count.limit=5 sensitive=false synonyms={DEFAULT_CONFIG:group.share.delivery.count.limit=5} share.partition.max.record.locks=2000 sensitive=false synonyms={DEFAULT_CONFIG:group.share.partition.max.record.locks=2000} share.record.lock.duration.ms=30000 sensitive=false synonyms={DEFAULT_CONFIG:group.share.record.lock.duration.ms=30000} pass 1 (release): received 0 pass 2 (release): received 60 <- 12 records x 5 deliveries, then archived pass 3 (release): received 0
    With 12 records and --release, one run received 60 = 12 × 5: each record was redelivered until the delivery count reached 5, then archived. (The first run joined the new group too late for its 5 s timeout.) This is Kafka’s built-in poison-message protection.
