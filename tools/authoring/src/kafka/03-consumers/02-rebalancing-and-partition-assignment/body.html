1 The story

Why does everyone stop when one pod restarts?

Before you start: 3.1 Consumers and consumer groups.

🤔 A third worker joined. Under one protocol, all 6 partitions were taken away. Under another, only 2. Same Kafka.

A rebalance moves partitions between consumers when someone joins or leaves. The old “eager” way stops everyone; the “cooperative” way and Kafka 4’s new consumer protocol (KIP-848) move only what must move. We ran all three on the same cluster with live traffic.

K> Sir, every time a kitchen pod restarts, all consumers stop for a moment. Why should pod 1 stop when pod 3 restarts? 2 Definition

Rebalancing and partition assignment

Formal definition (write this in exams)

A rebalance redistributes partitions among the members of a consumer group when membership or subscriptions change. In the classic protocol, members rejoin via the coordinator and the elected group leader computes an assignment using the configured partition.assignment.strategy; eager strategies revoke all partitions from all members first, while cooperative (incremental) strategies revoke only partitions that move, completing in additional rounds. In the consumer protocol (KIP-848, production-ready in Kafka 4.0) the group coordinator computes target assignments server-side and members reconcile incrementally through heartbeats.

In simple words

When consumers join or leave, Kafka re-shares the partitions. The old way makes everyone hand back everything; newer ways only move the partitions that need a new owner, so the rest keep working.

Why, how, where

Why

Members come and go (scaling, deploys, crashes). Partitions must be re-shared so none is orphaned and none is read twice.

the problem it solves

How

The group coordinator detects the change; partitions are revoked from some members and assigned to others, by the client (classic) or by the broker (consumer protocol).

the mechanism

Where

Autoscaling consumer pods, rolling deploys, crashed workers, new topics matching a pattern.

in real systems

When not

Frequent rebalances (flapping members, slow polls) hurt throughput; fix the cause instead of tuning around it.

Dissect an eager rebalance

LEVELS> Rebalancing at three levels L1> Reshuffling counters
  • A new cook joins. Eager: everyone leaves their counter, the manager reassigns all counters, everyone walks back. Cooperative: only two cooks hand over one counter each; the rest keep cooking.
L2> Classic protocol (client-side)
  • Triggers: a member joins, leaves, crashes (no heartbeat), exceeds the poll interval, or the subscribed topics change.
  • One member, the leader of the group, runs the assignment strategy (partition.assignment.strategy): range, roundrobin, sticky (eager) or cooperative-sticky (incremental, KIP-429).
  • Your hooks: on_revoke (commit offsets, flush state) and on_assign (load state). With cooperative mode you call incremental_assign/unassign.
L3> Consumer protocol (KIP-848, production-ready in Kafka 4.0)
  • Enable with group.protocol=consumer. The broker computes assignments (server-side assignors uniform or range) and sends each member its target through heartbeats.
  • Members reconcile independently: a partition is first revoked from its old owner, then given to the new one. No global “stop the world” barrier, no group-wide sync.
  • Client-side settings like partition.assignment.strategy and session.timeout.ms are replaced by broker/group configs (e.g. group.consumer.session.timeout.ms).
/LEVELS

Myth vs reality: things many websites still say

  • Myth: Every rebalance stops the whole group.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): Only eager strategies do; cooperative and KIP-848 move a few partitions.
    Proof: Steps 1–3: 6 vs 2 vs 2 partitions taken away.
  • Myth: The broker always decides assignments.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): In the classic protocol a client (the group leader) decides; in KIP-848 the broker does.
    Proof: Types table.
  • Myth: Rebalances only happen when consumers crash.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): Joins, leaves, slow polls and subscription changes also trigger them.
    Proof: Step 1: a join triggered it.
EXAM> “What is a rebalance and how do you reduce its impact?” Partition reassignment on membership change. Use cooperative-sticky or group.protocol=consumer (KIP-848) for incremental moves, static membership for restarts, commit in on_revoke, and keep polls within max.poll.interval.ms.

4 Step by step

One join, three protocols

All outputs are real: Kafka 4.4.0, 3 brokers; Python confluent-kafka 2.16.1. A producer sends 100 messages/s to 6 partitions; consumers A and B run; C joins after 14 s. Times are relative to C joining. Script: jobs/m3/l32_rebalance.py.

  1. Step 1 · Eager (classic + range)

    MODES["eager"] = {"group.protocol": "classic", "partition.assignment.strategy": "range"}
# A and B consume 6 partitions while a producer sends 100 msg/s; C joins after 14 s
    == eager: partitions revoked/assigned around C joining -0.01s B revoked [3, 4, 5] -0.01s A revoked [0, 1, 2] -0.00s A assigned [0, 1] +0.00s C assigned [4, 5] +0.00s B assigned [2, 3] partitions taken away from A and B: 6 of 6 A: longest gap between messages = 0.21 s B: longest gap between messages = 0.18 s
    At the moment C joined, A and B gave back all 6 partitions and received a fresh plan 10–20 ms later. B even changed from [3,4,5] to [2,3]. On our tiny topic the pause was a fraction of a second; with state to save, slow callbacks or many members it can be seconds to minutes.

  2. Step 2 · Cooperative-sticky

    MODES["cooperative"] = {"group.protocol": "classic", "partition.assignment.strategy": "cooperative-sticky"}
# callbacks use incremental_assign / incremental_unassign
    == cooperative: partitions revoked/assigned around C joining -0.00s B revoked [1] +0.00s A revoked [0] +3.01s C assigned [0, 1] partitions taken away from A and B: 2 of 6 A: longest gap between messages = 0.13 s B: longest gap between messages = 0.11 s
    Only one partition each left A and B. They kept processing the other two. A second round 3 s later handed the freed partitions 0 and 1 to C: two partitions were unowned for those 3 seconds, everything else ran on.

  3. Step 3 · The new consumer protocol (KIP-848)

    MODES["consumer"] = {"group.protocol": "consumer"}   # broker computes assignments
    == consumer: partitions revoked/assigned around C joining -4.06s A revoked [5] -4.05s B revoked [2] +0.00s C assigned [2, 5] partitions taken away from A and B: 2 of 6 A: longest gap between messages = 0.11 s B: longest gap between messages = 0.22 s
    The broker decided: A gave up 5, B gave up 2, then C received [2, 5]. Again only 2 of 6 moved. The revocations happened about 4 s before C’s assignment arrived on its next heartbeat: members reconcile one at a time, without a group-wide barrier.
