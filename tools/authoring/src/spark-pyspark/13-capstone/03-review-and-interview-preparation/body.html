2 Definition

A pipeline review

Formal definition (write this in exams)

A pipeline review verifies a data job against its non-functional requirements using observable evidence: the physical plan (explain, Spark UI) confirms join strategies, partition pruning and adaptive execution; output inspection confirms file counts and layout; controlled experiments compare alternatives (e.g. broadcast vs sort-merge under skew); and failure tests confirm that data-quality gates stop bad runs. A project narrative for interviews presents the same evidence as situation, task, actions with trade-offs, and measurable results.

In simple words

Check with real evidence that the pipeline is fast, writes tidy files, uses the right join and stops on bad data. Then explain the project as a short story with numbers, ready for interview follow-ups.

LEVELS> The review at three levels L1> A building inspection
  • The house stands, but the inspector checks wiring, plumbing and the fire exit. Your pipeline’s wiring is its plan; its fire exit is the quality gate.
L2> What to verify
  • Output layout: one file per partition, sensible sizes (Lesson 5.3).
  • Physical plan: the intended join strategy and partition pruning (Lessons 7.3, 8.2).
  • Behaviour under stress: skew, bad data, re-runs.
L3> Turning it into answers
  • STAR-style: Situation (TiffinGo, 842k messy orders), Task (revenue + churn features), Action (profile, medallion, tests, gates, broadcast), Result (0.59% quarantined, 25 s, identical on cluster).
  • Expect follow-ups that go one level deeper into each choice; every number above is your proof.
/LEVELS EXAM> “How do you verify a Spark job is efficient?” Read the physical plan (join types, exchanges, PartitionFilters, AQE final plan), check the Spark UI for stage times, shuffle sizes and skewed tasks, inspect the output layout for small files, and compare alternatives with measurements, not guesses.

4 Step by step

Reviewing the pipeline with evidence

All outputs are real: PySpark 4.3.0, local[4], on the capstone data from Lessons 13.1–13.2 (lab script jobs/m13/review.py).

  1. Step 1 · Output files

    valid.write.partitionBy("order_date").parquet(tmp1)                              # no repartition
valid.repartition("order_date").write.partitionBy("order_date").parquet(tmp2)  # pipeline
count_files(tmp1), count_files(tmp2)
    == 1) output files: with and without repartition(order_date) without repartition: 56 files for 14 days | pipeline (repartition first): 14 files
    Without repartition, each of the 4 tasks writes a file for every day it touches: 56 small files. Repartitioning by the partition column first puts each day in one task: 14 files. At a year of history that is 1,460 vs 365 files.

  2. Step 2 · The gold join plan and pruning

    plan = daily_city_revenue(so, rest)._jdf.queryExecution().executedPlan().toString()
"BroadcastHashJoin" in plan, "SortMergeJoin" in plan
so.filter("order_date = '2026-11-01'").explain()
    == 2) the gold join plan BroadcastHashJoin: True | SortMergeJoin: False | AdaptiveSparkPlan isFinalPlan=true: True filter on order_date -> PartitionFilters: [isnotnull(order_date), (order_date = 2026-11-01)]
    The plan confirms the broadcast join and AQE’s final plan. Filtering on the partition column shows PartitionFilters: Spark reads one day’s folder, not fourteen (Lesson 5.3).

  3. Step 3 · What if we had not broadcast?

    spark.conf.set("spark.sql.autoBroadcastJoinThreshold", -1)
for aqe in ("false", "true"):
    spark.conf.set("spark.sql.adaptive.enabled", aqe)
    timed(lambda: orders.join(rest, "rest_id").groupBy("city").sum("amount").collect())
    == 3) what if the restaurant join were not broadcast? (rest_id 1 has 30% of orders) sort-merge, AQE off : 5.30 s | SortMergeJoin True sort-merge, AQE on (tuned) : 1.12 s | SortMergeJoin True broadcast (pipeline) : 0.59 s
    Restaurant 1 has 30% of orders. A sort-merge join with AQE off took 5.30 s; AQE (coalescing and skew handling) brought it to 1.12 s; the broadcast in the pipeline needs no shuffle of orders at all: 0.59 s, about 9× faster than the naive plan.

  4. Step 4 · The gate on a bad day

    bad = raw.limit(3000).withColumn("amount", F.lit("N/A"))   # partner sends a broken export
day = raw_day.unionByName(bad)
_, q = clean_orders(day); pct = q.count() / day.count() * 100
print(pct, "STOP" if pct >= 2 else "ok")
    == 4) the quality gate stops a bad day quarantine_pct: 5.34 -> STOP: too many bad rows (gate < 2%)
    Injecting 3,000 “N/A” amounts into a day pushes the quarantine share to 5.34%, above the 2% gate. The job stops before gold, the dashboard keeps yesterday’s numbers, and on-call gets an alert.

Your 60-second project story

Situation: a food-delivery company needed daily city revenue and churn features from messy CSV/JSON exports. Task: build a reliable nightly PySpark pipeline. Action: profiled 842,587 orders and found duplicates, bad and negative amounts, orphans and a 30% skewed restaurant; designed landing/silver/gold with a quarantine; wrote pure transforms with 5 pytest tests; used try_cast for Spark 4 ANSI, a broadcast join for the skew, partitioned Parquet and quality gates. Result: 0.59% of rows quarantined with reasons, 25 s locally and identical results on a cluster, and a gate that stops bad days.

6 Real-world use

Where these reviews happen

1. Code review

Reviewers ask for the explain() output and test results with every pipeline change.

Pull request→plan + tests→Merge

2. Production readiness

Platform teams check file layout, gates, alerts and runbooks before go-live.

Checklist→evidence→Go-live

3. Cost reviews

Slow, shuffle-heavy jobs are found from the UI and fixed with broadcast or pruning.

Spark UI→experiments→Lower bill

4. Hiring loops

Data-engineering interviews pair a project deep-dive with Spark fundamentals.

Story→follow-ups→Offer

5. Incident reviews

After a bad-data incident, teams add a gate and a test so it cannot recur.

Incident→gate + test→Prevented
