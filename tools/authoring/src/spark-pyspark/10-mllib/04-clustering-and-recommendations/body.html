2 Definition

Clustering and recommendations

Formal definition (write this in exams)

Clustering partitions unlabelled data into groups of similar points; KMeans minimises within-cluster squared distances to k centroids. Collaborative filtering with Alternating Least Squares (ALS) factorises a sparse user–item rating matrix into low-rank user and item factors to predict missing ratings. FPGrowth mines frequent itemsets from transactions and derives association rules measured by support, confidence and lift.

In simple words

KMeans puts similar customers together, ALS guesses what a person will like from people with similar taste, and FPGrowth finds items that are often bought together.

Why, how, where

Why

Most business data has no labels: nobody tagged customers as “premium” or dishes as “goes with lassi”. These algorithms find structure that marketing, menus and apps can use.

the problem it solves

How

KMeans repeatedly assigns points to the nearest centre and moves centres (distance-based, so scale features). ALS factorises the sparse user × item rating matrix into user and item vectors by alternating least squares. FPGrowth counts frequent itemsets with an FP-tree and derives rules with confidence and lift.

the mechanism

Where

Customer segmentation, store clustering, “you may also like”, menu bundles, cross-sell, basket analysis; at a scale where a single machine is not enough.

in real systems

When not

KMeans assumes round clusters of similar size; ALS cannot score unseen users or items (cold start) and needs enough ratings per user; FPGrowth can explode with low minSupport.

Dissect an association rule

LEVELS> Clustering and recommendations at three levels L1> Seating guests
  • KMeans seats similar guests at the same table. ALS guesses which dish a guest will like from guests with similar taste. FPGrowth notices that whoever orders thali usually adds lassi.
L2> The API (pyspark.ml)
  • VectorAssembler → StandardScaler(withMean=True) → KMeans(k, seed); ClusteringEvaluator() gives the silhouette (−1…1, higher is better).
  • ALS(userCol, itemCol, ratingCol, rank, maxIter, regParam, coldStartStrategy, implicitPrefs); model.recommendForAllUsers(n), recommendForUserSubset; RegressionEvaluator(metricName="rmse").
  • FPGrowth(itemsCol, minSupport, minConfidence) → freqItemsets, associationRules (confidence, lift, support), transform for predictions.
L3> Practical details
  • Choose k by silhouette/elbow and business sense; also BisectingKMeans, GaussianMixture, LDA (topics).
  • ALS: user and item ids must be integers; coldStartStrategy="drop" for evaluation; implicitPrefs=True for clicks/views (confidence, not ratings); tune rank/regParam with CrossValidator (10.3). In our first try, users with ~4 ratings gave poor test RMSE: data per user matters.
  • Old RDD-based pyspark.mllib (ALS.train, KMeans.train) is in maintenance mode; use the DataFrame-based pyspark.ml.
/LEVELS

Myth vs reality: things many websites still say

  • Myth: KMeans works on raw columns.
    Reality (Spark 4.3): Scale first; distance is unit-sensitive.
    Proof: Step 1.
  • Myth: ALS can score anyone.
    Reality (Spark 4.3): Unknown users give NaN.
    Proof: Step 3.
  • Myth: High confidence means a real link.
    Reality (Spark 4.3): Check lift.
    Proof: Rule figure.
EXAM> “Which MLlib algorithms are unsupervised/recommendation?” KMeans (+ silhouette, scaling), BisectingKMeans, GaussianMixture, LDA; ALS (rank, regParam, maxIter, coldStartStrategy, implicitPrefs); FPGrowth (minSupport, minConfidence, lift). pyspark.ml (DataFrame) over pyspark.mllib (RDD).

All outputs are real: PySpark 4.3.0 MLlib, local[2], synthetic TiffinGo data generated with a fixed seed.

  1. Step 1 · Choose k

    vec = VectorAssembler(inputCols=["orders_per_month", "avg_spend"], outputCol="raw").transform(cust)
scaled = StandardScaler(inputCol="raw", outputCol="features", withMean=True).fit(vec).transform(vec)
for k in [2, 3, 4, 5]: ClusteringEvaluator().evaluate(KMeans(k=k, seed=1).fit(scaled).transform(scaled))

    Premium (₹651, 6 orders), frequent (15 orders, ₹219) and low-spend (4 orders, ₹183): ready for three marketing campaigns.
