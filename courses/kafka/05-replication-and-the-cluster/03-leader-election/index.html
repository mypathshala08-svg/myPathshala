Leader election

What happens when a leader broker dies mid-write? We kill it under load, time the failover, and use a preferred election to put leadership back. All outputs are real.

1 The story

Who takes over when a leader dies?

Before you start: 5.2 ISR.

🤔 We killed a leader broker in the middle of 160 writes. How many records were lost?

Zero: 160 of 160 delivered. The controller picked a new leader from the ISR, the producer refreshed its metadata and retried, and the longest pause was 9.8 s. Here is exactly what happened, why it took ~10 s, and how leadership comes back home afterwards.

  • Who chooses a new leader, and from which replicas.
  • Preferred, automatic and unclean elections, and when each happens.
  • Why failover took ~10 s, and how ELR (Kafka 4.x) makes elections safer.

At 2 a.m. a broker in TiffinGo’s cluster crashed. Orders kept flowing. Kabir wants to know who replaced the leader, how fast, and why the old broker did not get its leadership back.

Kabir

Sir, if broker 1 dies at 2 a.m., does someone have to wake up and pick a new leader?

Arjun Sir

No. The controller does it. It only chooses from the ISR, because those replicas have every committed record.

Riya

How fast?

Arjun Sir

Detection is the slow part: the controller waits for the broker’s session to time out (about 9 s). The switch itself is milliseconds. Let’s kill a leader while a producer writes, and time it.

2 Definition

Leader election

Formal definition (write this in exams)

Leader election is the controller’s choice of a new leader for a partition, normally from the live members of the ISR (or ELR) in replica-list order. It happens on leader failure, controlled shutdown, reassignment, and preferred-leader election (manual or automatic rebalancing). Each election increments the leader epoch, which fences stale leaders. An unclean election may select an out-of-sync replica when explicitly enabled, risking loss of committed records.

In simple words

When a partition’s leader disappears, the controller picks a fully caught-up copy to lead instead. Later it can hand leadership back to the “preferred” broker. Picking a copy that is behind is possible but can lose data, so it is off by default.

Why, how, where

Why

A partition with no leader cannot accept writes or serve reads. Elections restore service in seconds without a human.

the problem it solves

How

The active controller notices the broker is gone (missed heartbeats → fenced after broker.session.timeout.ms, 9 s default), picks the next live replica in the ISR as leader, bumps the leader epoch and writes a PARTITION_CHANGE record; brokers and clients follow.

the mechanism

Where

Every broker crash, restart, rolling upgrade (controlled shutdown moves leaders first), and preferred-leader rebalancing.

in real systems

When not

If no ISR member survives, the partition stays offline unless you allow an unclean election (possible data loss).

Where the 9.8 seconds went

Leader election at three levels

Pick your level. New to this? Start at Beginner and climb.

Beginner A new team captain

  • If the captain leaves the field, the coach picks the next player who knows all the plays (the ISR). Later, when the old captain returns and is fit, the coach may give the armband back (preferred election).

Intermediate The rules

  • The controller runs every election. A new leader is chosen from the ISR, in replica-list order.
  • Each election bumps the leader epoch; requests carrying an old epoch are rejected, so a returning old leader cannot write stale data (fencing).
  • Preferred election moves leadership back to the first replica. Automatic when auto.leader.rebalance.enable=true (default): every leader.imbalance.check.interval.seconds (300) if a broker’s imbalance exceeds leader.imbalance.per.broker.percentage (10). Manual: kafka-leader-election.sh --election-type PREFERRED.

Advanced When the ISR is empty

  • Unclean election (unclean.leader.election.enable=true, default false) lets an out-of-sync replica lead: availability now, but records it never copied are lost and offsets are truncated on others.
  • ELR (Eligible Leader Replicas, KIP-966, on in Kafka 4.x via eligible.leader.replicas.version): replicas that left the ISR without missing committed data (HW-safe) stay eligible, so many “ISR empty” cases recover cleanly instead of needing an unclean election.
  • Rolling restarts use controlled shutdown: the broker asks the controller to move its leaders away before stopping, so clients see almost no pause.

Myth vs reality: things many websites still say

  • Myth: A crashed leader loses unconfirmed writes.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): With acks=all + retries, producers resend; we lost 0 of 160.
    Proof: Step 3.
  • Myth: Leadership returns as soon as the broker restarts.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): Only after it rejoins ISR and a preferred election runs.
    Proof: Step 4: still 2.
  • Myth: Any replica can become leader.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): Only ISR/ELR members, unless unclean is enabled.
    Proof: Types table.

Exam tip: “What happens when a leader broker fails?” Controller detects (session timeout), elects next live ISR replica, bumps leader epoch, publishes metadata; clients get NOT_LEADER, refresh, retry. Preferred election restores balance; unclean election risks loss; ELR (KIP-966) widens safe choices.

7 Run the code

Run the code: a tiny controller

Elections in a few lines. Press Run, then try the challenges.

# Simplified controller: elect a new leader from the ISR
replicas = [1, 2, 3]
isr = [1, 2, 3]
leader, epoch = 1, 4

def fail(broker):
    global leader, epoch, isr
    isr = [b for b in isr if b != broker]
    if leader == broker:
        candidates = [b for b in replicas if b in isr]
        leader = candidates[0] if candidates else None
        epoch += 1
    print(f"broker {broker} down -> leader {leader}, epoch {epoch}, isr {isr}")

9 Quiz and revision

Check yourself

Quick revision

Lesson 5.3 in one screen

  • Controller elects new leader from ISR/ELR in replica order; epoch + 1.
  • Lab: killed leader 1 → leader 2, 160/160 delivered, 9.8 s gap (detection).
  • Preferred election returns leadership; auto every 300 s if imbalance > 10%.
  • Unclean election: availability over data; off by default.
  • Controlled shutdown moves leaders before stop.

Interview and viva questions

Can I make failover faster?

Lower broker.session.timeout.ms carefully; too low causes false fencing. Graceful shutdowns are near-instant anyway.

Do consumers notice?

They get NOT_LEADER errors, refresh metadata and continue from their offsets; no data is skipped.

What is leaderRecoveryState?

Shows whether a partition recovered after an unclean election (RECOVERING) or is normal (RECOVERED).

Is ELR on by default?

In Kafka 4.x clusters with eligible.leader.replicas.version 1, as in our lab.
