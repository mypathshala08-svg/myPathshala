ISR and the high watermark

1 The story

Ten records written, five visible

Before you start: 5.1 Brokers and replication and 2.4 acks.

🤔 The leader had 10 records. Consumers could see only 5. For almost ten seconds.

We froze one follower and wrote 5 records with acks=1. The leader stored them instantly, but consumers were not allowed to read them: the high watermark waited for every in-sync replica. Only when Kafka kicked the frozen follower out of the ISR did the watermark jump. That one rule is why consumers never read data that could be lost.

  • The difference between log end offset and high watermark.
  • Why a single slow follower can delay what consumers see.

A TiffinGo engineer reports that records acknowledged to the producer are not visible to consumers for several seconds. One broker has been having trouble. Riya and Kabir investigate.

Riya

Sir, the producer got “OK” for 5 records, but my consumer doesn’t see them. Is my consumer broken?

Arjun Sir

No. The producer used acks=1, so only the leader had to store them. But consumers may read only up to the high watermark: the point that every in-sync replica has reached.

Kabir

And one of our followers is stuck.

Arjun Sir

Exactly. Until Kafka removes it from the ISR, the watermark cannot move. Let’s freeze a follower and time it.

2 Definition

ISR and the high watermark

Formal definition (write this in exams)

The in-sync replica set (ISR) of a partition is the leader plus the followers that are sufficiently caught up; a follower that has not caught up within replica.lag.time.max.ms, or whose broker is fenced by the controller, is removed and is re-added once it catches up. The leader advances the high watermark (HW) to the minimum log end offset (LEO) across the ISR. Consumers can fetch only records below the HW, and acks=all produce requests complete only when the HW passes them, which guarantees that consumed and acknowledged records survive the loss of any ISR member except the last.

In simple words

The ISR is the list of copies that are up to date. The high watermark is the last position that every up-to-date copy has. Readers can only see data below it, so they never read something that might vanish if the leader dies.

Why, how, where

Why

If consumers read a record that only the leader has, and the leader dies, that record vanishes after failover: consumers would have seen “ghost” data.

the problem it solves

How

The leader tracks each follower’s fetch position. Followers that keep up form the ISR. The high watermark is the smallest log end offset in the ISR; consumers can read only below it. Laggards are dropped from the ISR so they cannot block progress forever.

the mechanism

Where

Every replicated partition; monitoring “under-replicated partitions” and ISR shrink/expand events is standard operations.

in real systems

When not

A slow follower still in the ISR delays the high watermark (and acks=all) until it is removed.

ISR and high watermark at three levels

Pick your level. New to this? Start at Beginner and climb.

Beginner A class that waits

  • The teacher (leader) writes 10 lines on the board. Notes are “official” only up to the line that every listed student has copied. If one student falls asleep, the teacher waits a little, then removes them from the list and moves on.

Intermediate The definitions

  • LEO (log end offset): next offset a replica will write. HW (high watermark): the minimum LEO across the ISR. Consumers fetch only offsets < HW.
  • ISR: the leader plus followers that are caught up. A follower that has not caught up for replica.lag.time.max.ms (30 s) is removed; it is added back when it catches up.
  • Under-replicated partition: ISR smaller than the replica list; the key replication health metric.

Advanced KRaft details

  • In KRaft a broker that stops heartbeating to the controller is fenced after broker.session.timeout.ms (9 s), and removed from every ISR at once: that is why our frozen follower left after about 9.6 s, not 30 s.
  • With acks=all, the leader answers the producer only when the HW passes the batch: a stuck in-sync follower delays acks too.
  • After a leader change, followers truncate any data above the new leader’s log using leader epochs (leader-epoch-checkpoint), so replicas never diverge.

Myth vs reality: things many websites still say

  • Myth: Consumers can read everything the leader has.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): Only up to the high watermark.
    Proof: Step 2: offsets 5–9 hidden for 9.6 s.
  • Myth: A follower leaves the ISR only after 30 s.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): In KRaft, a fenced broker (9 s session) leaves at once.
    Proof: Step 2: 9.6 s.
  • Myth: acks=1 records are immediately readable.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): They become readable once the ISR has them (HW).
    Proof: Step 2.

Exam tip: “What are ISR and high watermark?” ISR = leader + caught-up followers (lag < replica.lag.time.max.ms, broker not fenced). HW = min LEO of ISR; consumers read below HW; acks=all waits for HW; under-replicated = ISR < replicas.

Freeze a follower, watch the watermark

All outputs are real: Kafka 4.4.0, 3 brokers; topic hw-52b with replicas 1,2,3 (leader 1); follower k3 frozen with docker pause. Script: jobs/m5/l52_hw.py.

  1. Step 1 · Healthy partition

    sh("kafka-topics.sh --create --topic hw-52b --replica-assignment 1:2:3")
p.produce(T, value=...) x 5   # acks=1
c.get_watermark_offsets(TopicPartition(T, 0))   # (low, high watermark)
    == 1) healthy: 5 records, high watermark 5 ISR 1,2,3
    Five records; every replica has them, so HW = 5 and ISR = 1,2,3.

  2. Step 2 · Freeze a follower, write with acks=1

    subprocess.run(["docker", "pause", "k3"])
for i in range(5): p.produce(T, value=f"during-{i}")    # acks=1
p.flush()
# poll the high watermark every 0.25 s
    == 2) docker pause k3 (a follower). Write 5 more records with acks=1 (leader only) acks=1 confirmed after 0.00 s: the leader has 10 records (log end offset 10) t+ 0.0s high watermark 5 (consumers can read offsets < 5) t+ 9.6s high watermark 10 (consumers can read offsets < 10) ISR now 1,2
    The producer got its OK immediately (leader only). The HW stayed at 5 for 9.6 s: consumers could not see offsets 5–9. Then the controller fenced the frozen broker, the ISR shrank to 1,2, and HW jumped to 10.

  3. Step 3 · Recovery

    docker unpause k3
kafka-topics.sh --describe --under-replicated-partitions
    == 3) unpause k3: it catches up and rejoins the ISR high watermark 10 ISR 1,2,3 under-replicated partitions now: none
    k3 fetched the missing records and rejoined the ISR; no partition is under-replicated any more.

7 Run the code

Run the code: computing the high watermark

The HW rule in a few lines. Press Run, then try the challenges.

# High watermark = smallest LEO among ISR members
leo = {1: 10, 2: 10, 3: 5}          # broker -> log end offset
isr = {1, 2, 3}
lag_ms = {1: 0, 2: 0, 3: 35000}
MAX_LAG = 30000                      # replica.lag.time.max.ms (Kafka 4.x default)

def hw(isr):
    return min(leo[b] for b in isr)

print("HW with slow follower in ISR:", hw(isr))
isr = {b for b in isr if lag_ms[b] <= MAX_LAG}
print("ISR after lag check:", sorted(isr))
print("HW now:", hw(isr))
HW with slow follower in ISR: 5 ISR after lag check: [1, 2] HW now: 10
  • return min(leo[b] for b in isr)Committed = copied by every ISR member.
  • isr = {b for b in isr if lag_ms[b] <= MAX_LAG}A lagging follower is removed: HW can move again.

Interview and viva questions

Does ISR shrink lose data?

No; it removes a slow copy so writes can continue. Data is lost only if all ISR copies are lost.

Who decides ISR changes?

The partition leader proposes; in KRaft the controller records a PARTITION_CHANGE (AlterPartition).

What is ELR?

Eligible Leader Replicas (KIP-966): replicas that left ISR but are known to hold all committed data, so they can safely become leader.

Why did our follower leave after 9.6 s, not 30 s?

A paused broker also stops heartbeating to the controller; after broker.session.timeout.ms (9 s) it is fenced and removed from all ISRs at once. replica.lag.time.max.ms applies to a live but slow follower.

Why does HW lag on followers?

Followers learn the HW from the leader in the next fetch response, so it is a little behind.
