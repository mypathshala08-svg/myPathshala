2 Definition

Offsets, commits and lag

Formal definition (write this in exams)

Each consumer tracks a position, the offset of the next record to fetch per assigned partition. Committing persists a position as the group’s committed offset in the internal compacted topic __consumer_offsets, via the group coordinator; it is used to resume after restarts and rebalances. Commits can be automatic (periodic) or manual (synchronous or asynchronous). Consumer lag is the difference between a partition’s log end offset and the group’s committed offset. Administrators can change committed offsets with kafka-consumer-groups --reset-offsets while the group is inactive.

In simple words

The consumer remembers “next record to read” and saves that number in Kafka (a commit). After a restart it continues from there. Lag is how many records are waiting after that number.

Why, how, where

Why

A consumer will restart, crash or lose partitions. Without a saved position it would re-read everything or skip data.

the problem it solves

How

The group commits “next offset to read” per partition to __consumer_offsets; on restart or rebalance the owner resumes from there. Lag = log end offset − committed offset.

the mechanism

Where

Every consumer group; lag is the main health metric of any Kafka pipeline.

in real systems

When not

Committing does not mean processing succeeded; commit only after work is done (Lesson 3.4).

Dissect a commit record

The offset ruler again

Offsets and commits at three levels

Pick your level. New to this? Start at Beginner and climb.

Beginner A bookmark in a register

  • The billing clerk writes “done up to page 14” in a register. If someone else takes over, they start at page 15. Lag is how many pages are still unread.

Intermediate Position vs committed

  • Position: the next offset the consumer will fetch (in memory, moves with every poll).
  • Committed offset: the position saved to Kafka; used after restarts and rebalances. It is always “next to read”, not “last processed”.
  • enable.auto.commit=true commits the position every auto.commit.interval.ms (5 s) in the background; false means you call commit() yourself, synchronously (waits) or asynchronously (faster, no retry ordering).

Advanced Tools and storage

  • Commits are records in __consumer_offsets (50 partitions, compacted): key = group + topic + partition, value = offset, leader epoch, metadata, timestamp.
  • kafka-consumer-groups.sh --reset-offsets with --to-earliest, --to-latest, --to-datetime, --to-offset, --shift-by; always --dry-run first; the group must have no active members.
  • Committed offsets of an empty group expire after offsets.retention.minutes (7 days); then auto.offset.reset decides again.

Myth vs reality: things many websites still say

  • Myth: The committed offset is the last record processed.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): It is the next record to read (last processed + 1).
    Proof: Step 4: reset to 0 means “start at record 0”.
  • Myth: Offsets are stored in ZooKeeper or on the client.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): They are records in the __consumer_offsets topic.
    Proof: Step 5.
  • Myth: High total lag always means too few consumers.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): Often one partition is stuck or hot.
    Proof: Step 2: lag 189 on P0 vs 107 on P2.

Exam tip: “Explain consumer offsets and lag.” Position = next to fetch; committed offset = saved position in __consumer_offsets used on restart/rebalance; lag = log end − committed; auto vs manual commit trade-offs; reset with kafka-consumer-groups --reset-offsets (dry run, inactive group).

4 Step by step

Commit, wait, rewind

All outputs are real: Kafka 4.4.0, 3 brokers; Python confluent-kafka 2.16.1; topic orders-33 with 3 partitions; group billing-33. Script: jobs/m3/l33_offsets.py.

  1. Step 1 · Read 120, commit manually

    c = Consumer({"bootstrap.servers": BS, "group.id": "billing-33",
              "auto.offset.reset": "earliest", "enable.auto.commit": False})
...                                   # read 120 records
c.commit(asynchronous=False)
kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe --group billing-33
    == 1) billing reads 120 records and commits manually (enable.auto.commit=false) committed: [(0, 14), (1, -1001), (2, 106)] (-1001 = no commit yet) GROUP TOPIC PARTITION CURRENT-OFFSET LOG-END-OFFSET LAG CONSUMER-ID HOST CLIENT-ID billing-33 orders-33 0 14 100 86 - - - billing-33 orders-33 2 106 106 0 - - -
    The 120 records came from partitions 0 and 2 only, so partition 1 has no commit (-1001) and is not listed. Partition 2 is fully read (lag 0); partition 0 has 86 still waiting.

  2. Step 2 · The app is down, lag grows

    produce(300 more orders)
kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe --group billing-33
    == 2) the app is down while 300 more orders arrive GROUP TOPIC PARTITION CURRENT-OFFSET LOG-END-OFFSET LAG CONSUMER-ID HOST CLIENT-ID billing-33 orders-33 0 14 203 189 - - - billing-33 orders-33 2 106 213 107 - - -
    Committed offsets stay where they were (14 and 106) while log end offsets grow: lag 189 and 107. No data is lost; it is waiting.

  3. Step 3 · Plan a rewind (dry run)

    kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server k1:9092 --reset-offsets --group billing-33 \
  --topic orders-33:0,2 --shift-by -50 --dry-run
    == 3) shift back by 50 on the committed partitions (dry run: nothing changes yet) GROUP TOPIC PARTITION NEW-OFFSET billing-33 orders-33 2 56 billing-33 orders-33 0 0
    The dry run shows the new offsets without changing anything: 106 − 50 = 56, and 14 − 50 is clamped to 0.

  4. Step 4 · Reset to earliest (execute)

    kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server k1:9092 --reset-offsets --group billing-33 \
  --topic orders-33 --to-earliest --execute
    == 4) reset to the earliest offset (execute) GROUP TOPIC PARTITION NEW-OFFSET billing-33 orders-33 2 0 billing-33 orders-33 1 0 billing-33 orders-33 0 0
    All three partitions now start at 0: the next run of billing will re-read everything (useful after fixing a bug).

  5. Step 5 · Where the bookmark lives

    kafka-console-consumer.sh --bootstrap-server k1:9092 --topic __consumer_offsets --from-beginning \
  --formatter org.apache.kafka.tools.consumer.OffsetsMessageFormatter
    == 5) where commits are stored: __consumer_offsets (formatted) {"key":{"type":1,"data":{"group":"billing-33","topic":"orders-33","partition":2}},"value":{"version":4,"data":{"offset":0,"leaderEpoch":-1,"metadata":"","commitTimestamp":1793895436728,"topicId":"eMESfbWcTJe5Zj7xQk3G8g"}}} {"key":{"type":1,"data":{"group":"billing-33","topic":"orders-33","partition":1}},"value":{"version":4,"data":{"offset":0,"leaderEpoch":-1,"metadata":"","commitTimestamp":1793895436728,"topicId":"eMESfbWcTJe5Zj7xQk3G8g"}}} {"key":{"type":1,"data":{"group":"billing-33","topic":"orders-33","partition":0}},"value":{"version":4,"data":{"offset":0,"leaderEpoch":-1,"metadata":"","commitTimestamp":1793895436728,"topicId":"eMESfbWcTJe5Zj7xQk3G8g"}}}

7 Run the code

Run the code: position vs commit

Why commits matter after a crash. Press Run, then try the challenges.

# Position, commit and lag for one partition
log_end = 100            # next offset the producer will write
position = 0             # next offset this consumer will read
committed = None         # saved bookmark (None = never committed)

def process(n):
    global position
    position += n        # we read n records

def commit():
    global committed
    committed = position # "next to read", not "last read"

process(14); commit()
print("committed:", committed, "lag:", log_end - committed)
log_end += 103           # app down, more orders arrive
print("after outage lag:", log_end - committed)
process(50)              # read 50 more but crash before commit
print("restart resumes at:", committed, "(the 50 will be read again)")
committed: 14 lag: 86 after outage lag: 189 restart resumes at: 14 (the 50 will be read again)
  • committed = position # "next to read", not "last read"A commit saves the next offset to read.
  • print("restart resumes at:", committed, "(the 50 will be read again)")Uncommitted progress is re-processed after a restart.

9 Quiz and revision

Check yourself

Quick revision

Lesson 3.3 in one screen

  • Position (memory) vs committed offset (Kafka); committed = next to read.
  • Auto commit every 5 s vs manual sync/async; commit after processing.
  • Lag = log end − committed (lab: 189 and 107 after outage).
  • Reset: --dry-run first, inactive group; to-earliest/latest/datetime/offset/shift-by.
  • __consumer_offsets record: key group+topic+partition, value offset + metadata; compacted.
  • Offsets of empty groups expire after 7 days.

Interview and viva questions

Can I store offsets outside Kafka?

Yes, e.g. in the same database transaction as your results; on start use seek() to that offset. Spark Structured Streaming keeps offsets in its checkpoint.

Does commit() commit all partitions?

Without arguments it commits the current positions of all assigned partitions.

What is “time lag”?

How old the oldest unread record is; often more meaningful than a record count for alerts.

Can two groups share offsets?

No. Offsets are per group; a new group.id starts fresh according to auto.offset.reset.

Show a strong answer

The number of records between a partition’s log end offset and the group’s committed offset. Check with kafka-consumer-groups --describe or metrics; rising lag means consumers are falling behind.
