1 The story

“Sent” is not the same as “stored”

Before you start: Module 1, especially 1.4 and the lab from 1.6.

🤔 Your code said “sent” after 1 millisecond. Was anything actually sent?

No. In our lab produce() returned in about 1 ms, but the broker confirmed each record about 60 ms later. In between, your record travels through six layers you never see. This lesson opens all of them.

  • Why produce() is a promise, not a delivery.
  • Where your record waits, and how long it may wait before Kafka gives up.
  • The exact error you get when the broker cannot be reached.

TiffinGo’s order service logs “order sent to Kafka” and moves on. After a deployment, the finance team finds 2,000 paid orders that never reached the kitchen. Kabir is asked to find out why.

Kabir

Sir, I printed “order sent” right after produce(). But some orders never reached Kafka when the pod restarted.

Arjun Sir

Because produce() only puts the record in a queue inside your process. The real sending happens later, on a background thread. If your program exits first, those records die with it.

Riya

So how do we know a record really arrived?

Arjun Sir

The delivery callback. Kafka calls it with the partition and offset on success, or with an error. Let’s time every stage on our cluster.

2 Definition

How a producer sends a message

Formal definition (write this in exams)

A Kafka producer is a client that publishes records to topic partitions. Sending is asynchronous: send/produce serialises the key and value, determines the partition, and appends the record to a per-partition batch in the record accumulator. A background sender (I/O) thread transmits ready batches as Produce requests to each partition leader, handles retries for retriable errors, and completes each record’s future or invokes its delivery callback with the assigned offset or an error. A record that cannot be acknowledged within the delivery timeout fails.

In simple words

When you send, the producer only puts your record in a queue and returns. A background worker sends records in groups to the right broker and later tells you, through a callback, whether each one was saved.

Why, how, where

Why

Sending one network request per record would be slow. Producers queue records and send them in batches, in the background.

the problem it solves

How

produce() serialises, picks a partition and appends to an in-memory batch; a background thread sends batches to partition leaders and calls your callback with the result.

the mechanism

Where

Every app that writes to Kafka: order services, payment gateways, log shippers, IoT gateways.

in real systems

When not

Do not treat produce() as “saved”. If the app exits before flush(), queued records are lost.

The producer at three levels

Pick your level. New to this? Start at Beginner and climb.

Beginner A post office counter

  • You drop a letter in the box (produce) and walk away. The postman collects many letters, sorts them by route (partition) and delivers them in one trip (batch).
  • Later you get an SMS: “delivered” or “failed” (the callback).

Intermediate The six stages

  • Serializer: key and value objects → bytes (JSON, string, Avro…).
  • Partitioner: chooses the partition (Lesson 2.2).
  • Record accumulator: per-partition batches in memory, limited by a buffer (Java buffer.memory, librdkafka queue.buffering.max.kbytes).
  • Sender thread: sends ready batches to each partition’s leader, retries on retriable errors.
  • Callback / future: success with partition + offset, or an error.

Advanced Timeouts and back-pressure

  • A record may wait, be retried and be resent until a total deadline: Java delivery.timeout.ms (default 120 s), librdkafka message.timeout.ms (default 300 s). Then you get an error such as _MSG_TIMED_OUT.
  • request.timeout.ms limits one network request; a timeout there triggers a retry, not a failure.
  • If the buffer is full, Java blocks up to max.block.ms; librdkafka raises BufferError immediately: call poll() and try again.

Myth vs reality: things many websites still say

  • Myth: produce()/send() means the record is saved.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): It only queues the record; the callback confirms storage.
    Proof: Step 1: 5 produced, 0 callbacks until flush().
  • Myth: If sending fails, produce() raises an error.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): Network failures appear later in the callback.
    Proof: Step 3: _MSG_TIMED_OUT after 3 s, no exception.
  • Myth: Callbacks arrive in the order you sent.
    Reality (Kafka 4.4): They arrive per partition batch.
    Proof: Step 1: o1, o3, o4, then o2, o5.

Exam tip: “Explain the Kafka producer’s send path.” send() → serializer → partitioner → record accumulator (batch per partition) → sender thread → Produce request to the leader → acks → callback/future with offset or error; retries for retriable errors until delivery.timeout.ms; flush()/close() before exit.

Always call flush() (with a timeout) before your program exits, and call poll(0) regularly while producing so callbacks run and the queue drains. Never write “sent!” to a database before the callback says success.

7 Run the code

Run the code: queue now, deliver later

The producer’s two moments in a few lines. Press Run, then try the challenges.

# A tiny producer model: queue in your thread, send + callback later
import time
queue, log = [], []
def produce(key, value, callback):
    queue.append((key, value, callback, time.perf_counter()))   # returns at once
def flush():
    while queue:                                                # the "sender thread"
        key, value, cb, t0 = queue.pop(0)
        log.append(value)
        cb(None, key, len(log) - 1, (time.perf_counter() - t0) * 1000)
def report(err, key, offset, ms):
    print("delivered", key, "offset", offset, f"after {ms:.3f} ms" if not err else err)
for i in range(1, 4):
    produce(f"o{i}", f"order {i}", report)
    print("produce() returned, queue =", len(queue))
flush()
produce() returned, queue = 1 produce() returned, queue = 2 produce() returned, queue = 3 delivered o1 offset 0 after 0.029 ms delivered o2 offset 1 after 0.016 ms delivered o3 offset 2 after 0.018 ms
  • queue.append((key, value, callback, time.perf_counter())) # returns at onceproduce() only queues the record.
  • cb(None, key, len(log) - 1, (time.perf_counter() - t0) * 1000)The callback reports the offset: only now is it stored.

9 Quiz and revision

Check yourself

Quick revision

Lesson 2.1 in one screen

  • produce() = serialise + partition + append to a batch; returns in ~1 ms.
  • Sender thread sends batches to leaders; callback reports offset or error.
  • Lab: 5 queued, 0 callbacks until flush; 61 ms; median 34 ms, p99 56 ms.
  • Unreachable broker → _MSG_TIMED_OUT after message.timeout.ms (callback only).
  • Retriable vs non-retriable errors; delivery.timeout.ms 120 s (Java), message.timeout.ms 300 s (librdkafka).
  • Always flush() before exit; poll() regularly; handle BufferError.

Interview and viva questions

Is the Java producer thread-safe?

Yes. Share one producer per application; creating many producers wastes connections and memory. confluent-kafka Producer is also safe to share between threads.

Should I create a producer per request?

No. Create it once at startup and reuse it; creation fetches metadata and opens connections.

What is the difference between poll() and flush()?

poll(t) serves pending callbacks for up to t seconds; flush(t) polls until the queue is empty or t passes.

Why are callbacks out of order?

They complete per partition batch. Within one partition the order is preserved.

Can I send headers?

Yes: produce(..., headers=[("source", b"app")]). Headers carry metadata such as trace ids without changing the value.

Real-world scenario questions (asked in company interviews)

Producer questions.

Any company · backendFresher

“Is Kafka send() synchronous?”

Explain.

The interviewer is checking: answer?

Show a strong answer

No. send()/produce() queues the record and returns; a background thread sends batches. You get the result through a callback or Future. flush() waits for all pending records.

Avoid: “Yes, it writes to disk.”

FintechMid-level

Lost events

Events vanish during deployments.

The interviewer is checking: fix?

Show a strong answer
  • flush()/close() on shutdown.
  • Only acknowledge to users after the callback.
  • Monitor delivery errors.

Avoid: Blaming Kafka.

Data engineeringMid-level

Timeouts

Some records fail with timed out during a broker outage.

The interviewer is checking: explain?

Show a strong answer
  • They exceeded delivery/message timeout.
  • Retriable errors were retried until the deadline.
  • Persist failures; tune timeout.

Avoid: Setting retries=0.

PlatformSenior

Producer design

Design the producer layer for 50 services.

The interviewer is checking: design?

Show a strong answer
  • One shared, long-lived producer per service.
  • Standard config: acks=all, idempotence, linger, compression.
  • Metrics: queue, error rate, latency.

Avoid: A producer per request.
