On-premises vs cloud vs dedicated servers

Buy, rent a whole machine, or rent slices by the second? We compare four hosting options, see who manages which layer, and build a 3-year TCO with a real AWS Mumbai price.

1 The story

The ₹10 lakh quote

Before you start: 1.3, 1.4.

🤔 Buy servers, rent a dedicated server, or use the cloud — which one is cheaper and when?

It depends on how you use them. With a real AWS Mumbai price ($0.10605/h for an m7i.large) and clearly marked assumptions for hardware, power and staff, 4 servers for 3 years cost about ₹24 lakh owned, ₹9.4 lakh in the cloud running 24×7, and ₹3.2 lakh if you switch them off outside office hours. Getting them: 3–8 weeks vs 2 minutes.

2 Definition

On-premises vs cloud vs dedicated servers

Formal definition (write this in exams)

On-premises infrastructure is owned and operated by the organisation in its own facility; colocation places owned hardware in a provider’s facility; a dedicated server (bare metal) is a single physical server leased to one customer; cloud resources are virtualized, multi-tenant, self-service and metered. They are compared by total cost of ownership (TCO), provisioning time, control and compliance.

In simple words

Buy and run it yourself, rent one whole machine, or rent slices of the provider’s machines by the second.

Say it in an interview

30-second answer you can speak:

  • “On-premises means we own and run everything; colocation puts our servers in someone else’s data centre; a dedicated server is a whole physical machine rented only to us; the cloud gives virtual resources on demand, billed per use.”
  • “The choice depends on total cost of ownership, speed, control and compliance: cloud wins for variable or new workloads; steady 24×7 loads need reserved pricing, dedicated hosts or sometimes on-prem.”
  • “With a real AWS Mumbai price, four servers for three years were about ₹9 lakh in the cloud versus about ₹24 lakh owned with our assumptions, and new capacity took minutes instead of weeks.”

Exam tip: CapEx vs OpEx is asked in all three exams. AWS: Dedicated Hosts (BYOL, per-core licences) vs Dedicated Instances (hardware not shared, less visibility). Azure: Hybrid Benefit lowers licence cost. Compare TCO, not purchase price.

3 The big picture

Who manages which layer

All five options run the same app (the fifth, a managed service such as a managed database, is a preview of Module 2). The difference is who looks after each layer. Moving from left to right, the provider takes over more layers, and you pay for it as a service instead of buying it.

The more layers you hand over, the faster you can start and the less you buy up front — but the less control you have. That trade-off is the whole decision.

5 Explained part by part

The four options in one line each

On-premises

Your building, your servers, your staff. Full control, biggest up-front cost, slowest to change.

banks’ core systems

Colocation

Your servers in a provider’s data centre: they give space, power, cooling and network.

own kit, no building

Dedicated server (bare metal)

One whole physical server rented monthly; not shared with anyone.

steady loads, licences

Cloud VM

A virtual slice of a shared server, created in minutes and billed per second.

spiky, new, global

6 AWS · Azure · Google Cloud

The same idea in three clouds

Same idea in AWS, Azure and Google Cloud

Pick a cloud.

AWS: EC2 on-demand, Savings Plans / Reserved Instances for steady use, Dedicated Hosts and bare-metal (*.metal) instances, Outposts (AWS hardware in your data centre), Pricing Calculator and Migration Evaluator for TCO.

Azure: Virtual Machines, Reservations and Savings plan, Azure Dedicated Host, Azure Stack HCI / Azure Local (on-prem), Azure Hybrid Benefit for Windows/SQL licences, TCO calculator.

7 Real proof

A three-year TCO with a real price

The cloud price is real (AWS Mumbai price list, 9 Nov 2026); own-server costs are illustrative assumptions marked *.

  1. Step 1 · The real cloud price

    e = json.load(open("ec2_aps1_prices.json"))   # extracted from the AWS price list
h = e["m7i.large"]["usd_hour"]
    == 1) real price: m7i.large (2 vCPU, 8 GiB) Linux on-demand, Mumbai: $0.10605/h
    From Lesson 1.3’s Mumbai EC2 price file.

  2. Step 2 · Three options, three years

    own = hardware* + 36 × power/space* + 36 × staff*
cloud_247 = 4 × h × 24 × 365 × 3 × ₹84*
cloud_busy = 4 × h × 10 × 300 × 3 × ₹84*
    == 2) three ways to run 4 such servers for 3 years (₹, illustrative assumptions marked *) A) own servers (dedicated) ₹ 2,404,000 (hardware ₹1,000,000 up front*) B) cloud, running 24×7 ₹ 936,430 (nothing up front) C) cloud, office hours only ₹ 320,695 (stop at night and holidays)
    Assumptions (*) are illustrative; the cloud price is real.

  3. Step 3 · What the numbers say

    compare B vs A, C vs A
    == 3) what the numbers say B vs A: cloud cheaper by ₹1,467,570 C vs A: cloud cheaper by ₹2,083,305
    With these numbers cloud wins; with cheaper hardware or full-time heavy use, own/dedicated can win.

  4. Step 4 · Time to capacity

    typical lead time
    == 4) time to get 4 new servers (typical) own: purchase order + delivery + setup ≈ 3–8 weeks · cloud: ≈ 2 minutes (API call)
    Speed is often worth more than the cost difference.

9 Mistakes to avoid

Common mistakes, why they happen, how to fix them

Running cloud VMs 24×7 when used 10 h/day.Habit from owned servers.Schedule stop/start; use auto scaling.

Interview and viva questions

Is a cloud VM a dedicated server?

No, a VM shares a physical host; dedicated/bare-metal options exist for single-tenant needs.

When is on-prem better?

Very steady heavy loads with cheap power and existing staff, strict rules, or special hardware.

What is Outposts / Azure Local?

Provider hardware running in your own data centre, managed like the cloud (hybrid).

Why ₹84 per dollar?

A round assumption for illustration; use today’s rate.
