1 The story

“Delete all my data”

Before you start: 9.1 and 9.2.

🤔 A TiffinGo customer emails: “Delete all my data — my address, my orders, my phone number.” The data sits in a database, three S3 buckets, logs, analytics in BigQuery and last month’s backups. Can TiffinGo prove it was deleted everywhere?

That is privacy (people’s rights over their personal data) meeting compliance (proving you follow laws and standards). In India the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules (notified November 2025) now set those duties; banks follow RBI, cloud users follow CERT-In directions, and global customers bring GDPR, PCI DSS, HIPAA, ISO 27001 and SOC 2. This lesson explains seven frameworks one by one, animates an erasure request across a cloud system, and on your own account you detect personal data, set retention so data does not live forever, and keep audit logs as Indian rules require.

  • Privacy is about using data correctly; security is about protecting it.
  • Why “where is the data?” is the hardest compliance question in the cloud.
  • Certifications of the provider ≠ your compliance.

A loyal TiffinGo customer moved cities and asked the company to delete everything it held about her. The team deleted one database row — then realised her details were also in files, logs, analytics, backups and a marketing tool.

Riya

Sir, we deleted her row from the orders table. Done?

Arjun Sir

Her address is also in receipts, logs, BigQuery and backups. And our email tool has her phone number.

Kabir

But AWS is ISO 27001 certified — aren’t we compliant?

Arjun Sir

AWS’s certificate covers AWS’s half. Our compliance covers what we collect, why, where we keep it, for how long, and whether we can prove it.

Riya

So which rules apply to us?

Arjun Sir

DPDP for Indian customers, RBI if we touch payments data, CERT-In for incidents and logs, and GDPR for our Dubai and London customers. Let’s map them.

2 Definition

Privacy and compliance in the cloud

Formal definition (write this in exams)

Privacy in the cloud is the lawful, fair and transparent processing of personal data — collecting only what is needed, using it for stated purposes, protecting it and honouring individuals’ rights — while compliance is the ability to demonstrate adherence to applicable laws, regulations and standards (such as India’s DPDP Act and Rules, RBI and CERT-In directions, GDPR, ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS and HIPAA) through controls and evidence, with responsibilities shared between the cloud customer (data fiduciary/controller) and provider (processor).

In simple words

Using people’s data only in fair, legal ways — and being able to prove you follow the rules that apply to you.

Say it in an interview

30-second answer you can speak:

  • “Privacy is about lawful use of personal data — notice, consent, purpose, minimisation, rights; compliance is proving we meet laws like DPDP and standards like ISO 27001 with evidence.”
  • “In the cloud I map data, keep it in the right Region, set retention with lifecycle rules, keep 180-day logs for CERT-In, and use provider reports for their half.”

Type 1 · India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and Rules, 2025

Definition (say it in an interview): The DPDP Act, 2023 governs processing of digital personal data in India (and of Indians’ data by offerings abroad); data fiduciaries must give notice, obtain consent or rely on a legitimate use, limit processing to the purpose, take reasonable security safeguards, report breaches, erase data when no longer needed and honour data principals’ rights. The DPDP Rules, 2025, notified on 13 November 2025, phase most obligations in over about 18 months; breaches must be reported to the Data Protection Board, with a detailed report within 72 hours.

In plain words (no jargon): India’s law on how companies must collect, use, protect and delete people’s personal data.

Analogy: A tailor who takes your measurements only for the shirt you ordered, keeps them locked, and throws them away when you ask — and tells you if they get lost.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Notice. Itemised, clear language.
  2. Consent. Free, specific, withdrawable (consent managers possible).
  3. Safeguards. Encryption, access control, logs.
  4. Breach. Inform Board and affected people.
  5. Erasure. When purpose is served or consent withdrawn.

Words used above, in simple terms: data principal = the person the data is about · data fiduciary = the organisation deciding why/how data is processed · Significant Data Fiduciary = a fiduciary with extra duties (audits, DPO, impact assessments)

For the experienced — did you know? The Act allows the government to restrict transfers to specific countries (a “negative list”) rather than requiring all data to stay in India — but sector rules (like RBI’s) can still demand local storage.

Use it when: you process digital personal data of people in India — which almost every Indian app does.
Avoid / be careful when: you treat it as a privacy-policy text change only — it needs technical controls.

Type 2 · GDPR (EU General Data Protection Regulation)

Definition (say it in an interview): The GDPR regulates processing of personal data of people in the EU/EEA by organisations anywhere that offer them goods or services or monitor them; it requires a lawful basis, data minimisation, security, records of processing, impact assessments for high-risk processing, 72-hour breach notification and safeguards for transfers outside the EU.

In plain words (no jargon): Europe’s strict data protection law that also applies to foreign companies serving people in Europe.

Analogy: A strict building code in another country: if you build there — or sell houses there — you follow it, wherever your company is.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Map. Records of processing.
  2. Basis. Pick and document.
  3. DPIA. For high-risk processing.
  4. Transfers. SCCs / adequacy.
  5. Breach. 72-hour notification.

Words used above, in simple terms: DPIA = data protection impact assessment · SCCs = standard contractual clauses for transfers · adequacy = EU decision that a country protects data well enough

Type 3 · Indian sector rules (RBI, CERT-In, MeitY)

Definition (say it in an interview): Indian sector and cyber rules add specific cloud obligations: RBI’s 2018 direction requires payment system data to be stored only in India, and RBI outsourcing/IT guidelines govern regulated entities’ cloud use; CERT-In’s April 2022 directions require reporting specified cyber incidents within 6 hours and maintaining ICT system logs for 180 days within India; MeitY empanels cloud providers for government workloads.

In plain words (no jargon): Extra Indian rules: payment data in India (RBI), report hacks in 6 hours and keep logs 180 days (CERT-In).

Analogy: Local municipal rules on top of national building law: the national law sets the basics, the city adds its own fire and height rules.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Classify. Payment, health, personal data.
  2. Locate. Indian Regions where required.
  3. Logs. 180-day retention in India.
  4. Report. 6-hour CERT-In runbook step.
  5. Vendors. Contracts allow regulator audits.

Words used above, in simple terms: regulated entity = a bank, NBFC, payment operator etc. supervised by RBI · ICT logs = logs from information and communication systems · empanelment = official approval to supply to government

Use it when: you are a regulated entity, handle payments, or run ICT systems in India (CERT-In applies broadly).
Avoid / be careful when: logs are kept only 30 days by default — that breaks the CERT-In 180-day rule.

Type 4 · ISO/IEC 27001 (and 27017, 27018, 27701)

Definition (say it in an interview): ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard for an information security management system (ISMS): an organisation defines scope, assesses risks, applies controls (Annex A; 93 controls in the 2022 edition) and is certified by an accredited auditor; related standards add cloud security (27017), protection of personal data in public clouds (27018) and privacy management (27701).

In plain words (no jargon): An international certificate showing a company manages information security properly.

Analogy: A food-safety certification for a kitchen: an inspector checks that processes, hygiene and records are in place — and comes back every year.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Scope. Systems, locations, teams.
  2. Risks. Assess and treat.
  3. SoA. Statement of Applicability.
  4. Operate. Policies, training, monitoring.
  5. Audit. Internal + certification audits.

Words used above, in simple terms: ISMS = information security management system · Statement of Applicability = list of controls chosen and why · surveillance audit = yearly check between certifications

Type 5 · SOC 2

Definition (say it in an interview): A SOC 2 report is an independent auditor’s attestation (under AICPA standards) on a service organisation’s controls relevant to the Trust Services Criteria — security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality and privacy; Type 1 assesses design at a point in time and Type 2 tests operating effectiveness over a period (typically 3–12 months).

In plain words (no jargon): An auditor’s report proving a company’s security controls really worked over several months.

Analogy: A restaurant’s hygiene inspector visiting unannounced for six months and writing a report, instead of checking once on opening day.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Scope. System and criteria.
  2. Readiness. Gap assessment.
  3. Operate. Collect evidence continuously.
  4. Audit. Type 1, then Type 2.
  5. Share. With customers under NDA.

Words used above, in simple terms: attestation = auditor’s formal opinion · Trust Services Criteria = the AICPA control categories · SOC 3 = a public, summary version

Type 6 · PCI DSS

Definition (say it in an interview): The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS), currently version 4.0.1, sets security requirements for any organisation that stores, processes or transmits payment card data — network segmentation, encryption, access control, logging, vulnerability management and regular testing — assessed annually by a Qualified Security Assessor or self-assessment questionnaire.

In plain words (no jargon): Security rules for any business that handles credit or debit card details.

Analogy: Handling cash in a shop: keep it in one locked till, few people with keys, counted and audited regularly — or use a card machine so you never touch cash.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Avoid. Hosted checkout + tokens.
  2. Segment. Isolated CDE if you must store.
  3. Encrypt. In transit and at rest.
  4. Log. All access to card data.
  5. Test. Scans and pentests.

Words used above, in simple terms: PAN = primary account number — the card number · CDE = cardholder data environment · tokenisation = replacing card data with a meaningless token

Type 7 · HIPAA (US health data)

Definition (say it in an interview): HIPAA is a US law whose Privacy and Security Rules protect protected health information (PHI); covered entities and their business associates — including cloud providers storing PHI — must sign business associate agreements (BAAs) and apply administrative, physical and technical safeguards such as access control, audit logs and encryption.

In plain words (no jargon): A US law that protects patients’ health information, including when it is stored in the cloud.

Analogy: A hospital’s medical-records room: only authorised staff, every access logged, and every outside contractor signs a confidentiality agreement.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Identify PHI. What and where.
  2. BAA. With provider and vendors.
  3. Eligible services. Only those.
  4. Safeguards. Access, audit, encryption.
  5. Breach. Notification rules.

Words used above, in simple terms: PHI = protected health information · BAA = business associate agreement · covered entity = hospital, insurer, clearinghouse

6 Explained part by part

Six building blocks of cloud privacy

Data inventory / map

What it is: Where personal data lives and flows.

Why it exists: Needed for rights, breaches and audits.

Where it is used: Macie, Purview, Sensitive Data Protection.

Classification

What it is: Labels: public, internal, personal, sensitive.

Why it exists: Drives controls and retention.

Where it is used: Tags/labels on buckets and tables.

Retention policy

What it is: How long each data type is kept.

Why it exists: Minimisation; erasure by default.

Where it is used: Lifecycle rules, log retention.

Data residency

What it is: Where data is stored and processed.

Why it exists: RBI, government, customer contracts.

Where it is used: Region choice + guard-rails (3.1).

Processor contracts

What it is: DPAs, BAAs with vendors.

Why it exists: Legal basis to use cloud and SaaS.

Where it is used: Provider data processing terms.

Evidence

What it is: Logs, reports, records of requests.

Why it exists: Compliance must be provable.

Where it is used: Audit logs, request register, reports.

Myth vs reality: things many websites still say

  • Myth: Provider certification = our compliance.
    Reality: It covers the provider’s scope only.
    Proof: Type 4.
  • Myth: Keeping data forever is harmless.
    Reality: It breaks minimisation and grows breach impact.
    Proof: Step 2.
  • Myth: Data in an Indian Region is DPDP-compliant.
    Reality: Residency is one control among many.
    Proof: Type 1.

9 Hands-on

Do it yourself, step by step

These labs run on your own cloud account — we cannot run them for you. Each step has three tabs (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud); pick the cloud you have and do both the UI way and the CLI way. Stay inside free tier / credit limits, use the India Region (AWS ap-south-1 Mumbai · Azure Central India · Google asia-south1 Mumbai), and always do the Clean up step at the end. You find personal data in text, make data expire automatically, and keep audit logs for 180 days in India. Costs are a few rupees at most.

  1. 0 · Get ready: account, budget, CLI

    Pick your cloud.

    UI way — AWS Management Console

    1. Create an AWS account (aws.amazon.com → Create an AWS Account); choose the Free plan while learning.
    2. Never work as root: IAM Identity Center (or IAM) → create an admin user with MFA and sign in as that user.
    3. Billing and Cost Management → Budgets → Create budget → monthly cost budget, e.g. $5, alert by e-mail (Lesson 5.5).
    4. Top-right Region menu → Asia Pacific (Mumbai). Open CloudShell (terminal icon) — the AWS CLI is already signed in there.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    aws sts get-caller-identity          # who am I? (must NOT be the root user)
aws configure set region ap-south-1
aws configure list

    You should see: your account ID and an IAM user/role ARN — not “root”.

    UI way — Azure portal

    1. Create a free account at azure.microsoft.com/free (credit for the first 30 days).
    2. Search Cost Management → Budgets → Add → monthly budget, e.g. ₹400, alert at 80%.
    3. Open Cloud Shell (the >_ icon in the top bar) → choose Bash. The Azure CLI is already signed in.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    az account show --output table        # subscription in use
az account list-locations --query "[?name=='centralindia'].displayName" -o tsv

    You should see: your subscription name and state “Enabled”, then “Central India”.

    UI way — Google Cloud console

    1. Sign in at console.cloud.google.com and start the free trial (credit for 90 days).
    2. Create a project: project picker → New project → name tiffingo-lab.
    3. Billing → Budgets & alerts → Create budget → e.g. ₹400, alerts at 50/90/100%.
    4. Click Activate Cloud Shell (>_ icon). The gcloud CLI is already signed in.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    gcloud config set project tiffingo-lab      # use your real project ID
gcloud config set compute/region asia-south1
gcloud config list

    You should see: your account e-mail, the project and region asia-south1.

    Cloud Shell is the easiest CLI: free, in the browser, already logged in. On your laptop install the AWS CLI v2, Azure CLI or Google Cloud CLI and run aws configure / az login / gcloud init.

  2. 1 · Detect personal data in text (data discovery basics)

    Pick your cloud.

    UI way — AWS Management Console

    1. Amazon Comprehend → Real-time analysis → analysis type Personally identifiable information (PII) → paste the sample sentence → Analyze.
    2. For whole buckets, Amazon Macie scans S3 for PII (30-day trial).

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    aws comprehend detect-pii-entities --language-code en --region ap-south-1 \
  --text "Order 1043 for Priya Sharma, phone 9876543210, email priya.sharma@example.com, deliver to 12 MG Road, Pune." --query 'Entities[].[Type,Score]' --output table

    You should see: rows such as NAME, PHONE, EMAIL, ADDRESS with confidence scores near 0.99. Cost: a fraction of a rupee for this text.

    UI way — Azure portal

    1. Create a Language resource on the free F0 tier → open Language Studio → Extract PII → paste the sentence → Run.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    az group create -n rg-privacy -l centralindia
az cognitiveservices account create -n tglang$RANDOM -g rg-privacy -l centralindia --kind TextAnalytics --sku F0 --yes
N=$(az cognitiveservices account list -g rg-privacy --query [0].name -o tsv)
EP=$(az cognitiveservices account show -n $N -g rg-privacy --query properties.endpoint -o tsv)
KEY=$(az cognitiveservices account keys list -n $N -g rg-privacy --query key1 -o tsv)
curl -s "${EP}language/:analyze-text?api-version=2023-04-01" -H "Ocp-Apim-Subscription-Key: $KEY" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"kind":"PiiEntityRecognition","analysisInput":{"documents":[{"id":"1","language":"en","text":"Order 1043 for Priya Sharma, phone 9876543210, email priya.sharma@example.com, deliver to 12 MG Road, Pune."}]}}' \
  | python3 -c "import sys,json; d=json.load(sys.stdin)['results']['documents'][0]; print(d['redactedText']); [print(e['category'], e['text']) for e in d['entities']]"

    You should see: the redacted sentence (Order 1043 for ***********, phone **********…) and categories like Person, PhoneNumber, Email, Address. F0 allows a few thousand records a month free (one free Language resource per subscription).

    UI way — Google Cloud console

    1. Security → Sensitive Data Protection → Inspection — or use the API from Cloud Shell (right). Discovery can profile whole BigQuery datasets and buckets.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    gcloud services enable dlp.googleapis.com
P=$(gcloud config get-value project)
curl -s -X POST "https://dlp.googleapis.com/v2/projects/$P/locations/asia-south1/content:inspect" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $(gcloud auth print-access-token)" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"item":{"value":"Order 1043 for Priya Sharma, phone 9876543210, email priya.sharma@example.com, deliver to 12 MG Road, Pune."},"inspectConfig":{"infoTypes":[{"name":"PERSON_NAME"},{"name":"PHONE_NUMBER"},{"name":"EMAIL_ADDRESS"},{"name":"STREET_ADDRESS"}],"includeQuote":true}}' \
  | python3 -c "import sys,json; [print(f['infoType']['name'], f.get('quote'), f['likelihood']) for f in json.load(sys.stdin)['result'].get('findings',[])]"

    You should see: lines like PERSON_NAME Priya Sharma LIKELY, EMAIL_ADDRESS priya.sharma@example.com VERY_LIKELY. Small inspections fall within the free monthly allowance.

    This is how data maps are built at scale: scanners find personal data in buckets, tables and logs you forgot about.

  3. 2 · Make data expire automatically (retention = minimisation)

    Pick your cloud.

    UI way — AWS Management Console

    1. S3 → create bucket tiffingo-raw-… → Management → Create lifecycle rule: prefix raw-orders/, Expire current versions after 30 days.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    B=tiffingo-raw-$RANDOM; aws s3 mb s3://$B --region ap-south-1
aws s3api put-bucket-lifecycle-configuration --bucket $B --lifecycle-configuration \
  '{"Rules":[{"ID":"raw-orders-30d","Status":"Enabled","Filter":{"Prefix":"raw-orders/"},"Expiration":{"Days":30}}]}'
aws s3api get-bucket-lifecycle-configuration --bucket $B --query 'Rules[].[ID,Expiration.Days]' --output text

    You should see: raw-orders-30d 30 — raw order exports now delete themselves after 30 days; nobody has to remember.

    UI way — Azure portal

    1. Storage account (Central India) → Lifecycle management → Add a rule: filter raw-orders/, Delete the blob if last modified more than 30 days ago.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    SA=tgraw$RANDOM; az storage account create -n $SA -g rg-privacy -l centralindia --sku Standard_LRS
cat > policy.json <<'EOF'
{"rules":[{"enabled":true,"name":"raw-orders-30d","type":"Lifecycle","definition":{"filters":{"blobTypes":["blockBlob"],"prefixMatch":["raw-orders/"]},"actions":{"baseBlob":{"delete":{"daysAfterModificationGreaterThan":30}}}}}]}
EOF
az storage account management-policy create --account-name $SA -g rg-privacy --policy @policy.json
az storage account management-policy show --account-name $SA -g rg-privacy --query "policy.rules[].name" -o tsv

    You should see: raw-orders-30d.

    UI way — Google Cloud console

    1. Cloud Storage → bucket in asia-south1 → Lifecycle → Add a rule: delete object, condition age 30 days, prefix raw-orders/.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    B=tiffingo-raw-$RANDOM; gcloud storage buckets create gs://$B --location=asia-south1
cat > lc.json <<'EOF'
{"rule":[{"action":{"type":"Delete"},"condition":{"age":30,"matchesPrefix":["raw-orders/"]}}]}
EOF
gcloud storage buckets update gs://$B --lifecycle-file=lc.json
gcloud storage buckets describe gs://$B --format="value(lifecycle_config)"

    You should see: the rule with age: 30 and the prefix.

    DPDP and GDPR both expect erasure when the purpose is served — lifecycle rules make that the default.

  4. 3 · Keep audit logs for 180 days in India (CERT-In)

    Pick your cloud.

    UI way — AWS Management Console

    1. CloudTrail → Trails → Create trail india-logs → new S3 bucket in Mumbai → management events read + write → Create.
    2. Add a lifecycle rule on that bucket: move to Glacier after 30 days, expire after 180 days or later per your policy.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    LB=tiffingo-trail-$RANDOM; aws s3 mb s3://$LB --region ap-south-1
ACC=$(aws sts get-caller-identity --query Account --output text)
aws s3api put-bucket-policy --bucket $LB --policy "{\"Version\":\"2012-10-17\",\"Statement\":[{\"Effect\":\"Allow\",\"Principal\":{\"Service\":\"cloudtrail.amazonaws.com\"},\"Action\":\"s3:GetBucketAcl\",\"Resource\":\"arn:aws:s3:::$LB\"},{\"Effect\":\"Allow\",\"Principal\":{\"Service\":\"cloudtrail.amazonaws.com\"},\"Action\":\"s3:PutObject\",\"Resource\":\"arn:aws:s3:::$LB/AWSLogs/$ACC/*\",\"Condition\":{\"StringEquals\":{\"s3:x-amz-acl\":\"bucket-owner-full-control\"}}}]}"
aws s3api put-bucket-lifecycle-configuration --bucket $LB --lifecycle-configuration \
  '{"Rules":[{"ID":"keep-180d","Status":"Enabled","Filter":{},"Transitions":[{"Days":30,"StorageClass":"GLACIER_IR"}],"Expiration":{"Days":180}}]}'
aws cloudtrail create-trail --name india-logs --s3-bucket-name $LB --region ap-south-1
aws cloudtrail start-logging --name india-logs --region ap-south-1
aws cloudtrail get-trail-status --name india-logs --region ap-south-1 --query IsLogging

    You should see: true. The first trail of management events is free (you pay only S3 storage). Logs stay in Mumbai, move to a cheaper tier after 30 days and are kept 180 days.

    UI way — Azure portal

    1. Monitor → Activity log → Export Activity Logs / Diagnostic settings → Archive to a storage account in Central India → add a lifecycle rule deleting after 180 days.
    2. (Activity log itself keeps 90 days — exporting is what makes 180 possible.)

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    LSA=tglogs$RANDOM; az storage account create -n $LSA -g rg-privacy -l centralindia --sku Standard_LRS
SUB=$(az account show --query id -o tsv)
az monitor diagnostic-settings subscription create -n india-logs --location centralindia \
  --storage-account $(az storage account show -n $LSA -g rg-privacy --query id -o tsv) \
  --logs '[{"category":"Administrative","enabled":true},{"category":"Security","enabled":true},{"category":"Policy","enabled":true}]'
az monitor diagnostic-settings subscription list --query "value[].name" -o tsv

    You should see: india-logs; activity logs now land in a Central India storage account (add a 180-day lifecycle rule as in step 2).

    UI way — Google Cloud console

    1. Logging → Logs Storage → _Default bucket → Edit bucket → retention 180 days. (Admin Activity audit logs in _Required are kept 400 days automatically.)
    2. For Indian data residency of logs, create a log bucket in asia-south1 and route logs there with the Log Router.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    gcloud logging buckets update _Default --location=global --retention-days=180
gcloud logging buckets list --format="table(name.basename(),location,retentionDays)"

    You should see: _Default global 180 and _Required global 400. Storage beyond the first 30 days is billed per GB-month — small for a lab project.

    Retention cuts both ways: delete personal data you no longer need (step 2), keep security logs as long as rules require (step 3).

Clean up and cost check

AWS: aws cloudtrail delete-trail --name india-logs --region ap-south-1 (or keep it); aws s3 rb s3://$B --force; aws s3 rb s3://$LB --force. Azure: az monitor diagnostic-settings subscription delete -n india-logs --yes; az group delete -n rg-privacy --yes --no-wait. Google Cloud: gcloud storage rm -r gs://$B; set _Default back to 30 days if you prefer: gcloud logging buckets update _Default --location=global --retention-days=30.

10 Mistakes to avoid

Common mistakes, why they happen, how to fix them

Mistake or symptomWhy it happensHow to fix or avoid
“Our provider is certified, so we are compliant.”Confusing their scope with yours.Use their reports for their half; certify your own controls.
No data map.Data copied everywhere over years.Discovery scans + inventory per system.
Keeping everything forever.Storage is cheap.Retention policy per data type; lifecycle rules.
Personal data in logs and analytics.Debug logging, raw exports.Mask/pseudonymise before logging; aggregate analytics.
Default 30–90 day logs.Not configured.Export and retain 180 days in India (CERT-In).
Card numbers in your systems.Custom checkout.Hosted payment page + tokens.

Interview and viva questions

Is storing data in Mumbai enough for DPDP?

No — residency is one control; consent, purpose, security, rights and breach duties remain.

Who is the processor in cloud?

Usually the cloud provider and SaaS tools acting on your instructions.

Why keep logs but delete personal data?

Different rules: security logs have mandated retention; personal data must be minimised.
