Data quality, governance, security and the DPDP Act

A pipeline can run perfectly and still publish wrong numbers, and a single shared sheet can leak lakhs of phone numbers. In this lesson you learn the six data quality dimensions, build a quality gate that quarantines bad rows, trace a 100× revenue error with lineage, see one table masked differently for each role, and handle an erasure request and a breach under India’s DPDP Act.

1 The story

Every job was green

On Monday morning TiffinGo’s revenue dashboard shows ₹38.6 crore for Sunday, a hundred times the usual figure. The CEO forwards it to the whole leadership group with one line: “Is this real?” By noon Kabir finds the cause: the payments team started sending amounts in paise instead of rupees, and nothing in the pipeline checked. On Wednesday a vendor reports that a spreadsheet with two lakh customer phone numbers is open to anyone with the link.

Kabir

Sir, the pipeline didn’t fail. Every job was green. That’s what scares me.

Arjun Sir

That’s the lesson, Kabir. A pipeline can run perfectly and still deliver wrong data. Data quality checks catch that. And knowing who owns each table, where it came from and who may see it is governance.

Riya

And the phone numbers? That sounds like a security problem, not a data one.

Arjun Sir

It’s both, Riya. Personal data needs protection by design: masking, access control, encryption, audit logs. In India the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 also makes it a legal duty, with penalties of up to ₹250 crore.

Kabir

So quality, governance, security and privacy are four separate jobs?

Arjun Sir

Four sides of one promise: the right data, correct, to the right people, used lawfully. Today you’ll build a quality gate, trace a broken number to its source, mask personal data, and handle a deletion request and a breach the DPDP way.

2 Definition

Data quality, governance, security and privacy

Formal definition (write this in exams)

Data quality is the degree to which data is fit for its intended use, measured along dimensions such as accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, validity and uniqueness. Data governance is the set of roles, policies, standards and processes that ensure data is managed as an asset: ownership and stewardship, metadata and catalogs, lineage, classification, retention and access policies. Data security protects data against unauthorised access, modification and loss through identity and access management, encryption, network controls, monitoring and auditing. Data privacy governs the lawful processing of personal data, in India under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, 2025.

In simple words

Quality: is the data correct? Governance: who owns it, what does it mean, who may use it? Security: is it locked so only the right people can reach it? Privacy: are we using people’s personal data fairly, with their permission, and as the law requires?

Words you’ll meet in this lesson

PIIPersonally identifiable information: data that identifies a person, such as name, phone, email, Aadhaar or address.
Data owner / stewardThe owner is accountable for a dataset; the steward manages its definitions and quality day to day.
Data catalogA searchable inventory of datasets with owners, descriptions, schemas, tags and usage.
LineageThe path data takes from sources through transformations to reports and models.
QuarantineHolding bad rows aside for review instead of loading them into trusted tables.
MaskingHiding part or all of a value (XXXXXX8843) for users who don’t need the full value.
PseudonymisationReplacing identifiers with tokens or salted hashes so data can be joined without exposing identity.
Data fiduciary / principalUnder DPDP: the organisation deciding how personal data is processed / the person the data is about.

DPDP Act and Rules: the key facts (as of October 2026)

TimelineData Protection Board set up first; consent-manager provisions from about November 2026; most obligations from about May 2027 (the government has consulted on shortening this for large companies)

Quality, governance and security at three levels

Pick your level. New to this? Start at Beginner and climb.

Beginner Trustworthy data, simply

  • Data quality means the data is correct and usable: no missing values where needed, no duplicates, sensible ranges, delivered on time.
  • Governance means clear rules: who owns each dataset, what each column means, who may use it, how long it is kept.
  • Security means only allowed people and programs can read or change data, and every access is logged.
  • Privacy means personal data (name, phone, email, location, payments) is collected with consent, used only for the stated purpose and deleted when no longer needed.

Intermediate How teams put it into practice

  • Quality dimensions: accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness (freshness), validity (format and range) and uniqueness.
  • Quality checks in pipelines: tests on every load (row counts, nulls, ranges, uniqueness, referential integrity), with bad rows quarantined and the pipeline stopped above a threshold. Tools: Great Expectations, dbt tests, Soda, Deequ, Delta Live Tables expectations.
  • Governance tools: a data catalog (what exists, owner, meaning), lineage (where data came from and where it goes), classification (tagging PII and sensitive columns) and data contracts between producers and consumers.
  • Security controls: role-based access (RBAC), column masking and row-level filters, encryption at rest and in transit, secrets in a vault, private networks, audit logs.

Advanced Privacy law, observability and hard problems

  • DPDP Act, 2023 (India): data principals (people) and data fiduciaries (companies); consent that is free, specific and informed, with notice; purpose limitation; rights to access, correction and erasure; reasonable security safeguards; breach notification to the Data Protection Board and affected people; extra duties for children’s data and for Significant Data Fiduciaries.
  • Erasure at big data scale: deleting one person from a lake means deleting from tables, derived tables, caches, ML features and eventually backups. Table formats (Delta, Iceberg, Hudi) support row-level deletes; pseudonymous keys and separate PII stores make it manageable.
  • Pseudonymisation vs anonymisation: replacing an email with a salted hash is pseudonymisation (still personal data if it can be linked back); true anonymisation is hard because combinations of columns can re-identify people.
  • Data observability: monitoring freshness, volume, schema and distribution changes automatically, with alerts, rather than relying only on hand-written tests.
  • Data mesh and contracts: domain teams own their data products, publish schemas and SLAs as contracts, and breaking changes are caught before deployment.

4 Step by step

Making a dataset trustworthy

  1. Classify the data

    Kabir tags every column in the catalog: phone, email, address as PII; amount as financial; city as internal.

    You can’t protect what you haven’t labelled. Tags drive masking, access policies and retention automatically.

  2. Write quality rules with the owner

    rows = [120, 450, -150, 300, 0, 99000]
bad = [r for r in rows if not 1 <= r <= 50_000]
print("bad:", bad, f"-> {len(bad) / len(rows):.0%} failed")
    bad: [-150, 0, 99000] -> 50% failed
    Rules come from business meaning, so the data owner signs off: “amount between ₹1 and ₹50,000”, “one row per order”, “ready by 6 am”.

  3. Gate the pipeline

    Checks run after every load. Bad rows go to a quarantine table; if more than 2% fail, the pipeline stops and alerts the owner instead of publishing.

    A stopped pipeline with an alert is far better than a confident wrong dashboard.

  4. Mask and restrict

    phone = "9876543210"
print("masked:", "XXXXXX" + phone[-4:])
    masked: XXXXXX3210
    Analysts get masked phones and pseudonymous customer keys; only the support tool can see full numbers, and every lookup is logged.

  5. Record lineage and ownership

    Each table in the catalog shows its owner, description, upstream sources and downstream dashboards, captured automatically from pipeline code where possible.

    When a number looks wrong, lineage turns hours of guessing into minutes of tracing.

  6. Plan for rights and breaches

    A runbook says how to find and delete one customer everywhere, how long each table is kept, and who notifies the Data Protection Board and customers if a breach happens.

    DPDP turns these from good ideas into legal duties with deadlines.

What Kabir sees, and how the data moved 6 steps
What you see
  1. The dashboard shows ₹38.6 crore for Sunday, about 100 times normal.
  2. Kabir opens the gold table in the catalog and clicks Lineage.
  3. He follows it upstream to the payments team’s orders table.
  4. The cause: a schema change on Saturday sent amounts in paise.
  5. After the fix and backfill, Sunday shows ₹38.6 lakh.
  6. A new quality check and a data contract protect the table from now on.
Behind the screen
  1. Every job succeeded, so no alert fired. Pipeline monitoring checks that jobs ran, not that values make sense.
  2. The catalog’s lineage graph shows gold.revenue is built from silver.orders, which is built from bronze.orders. The sums agree between silver and gold, so the transformation is not the problem.
  3. Comparing distributions shows the median order amount jumped from about 320 to 32,000 on Saturday, already in the raw bronze copy.
  4. The payments team switched the amount field to paise (a common way to avoid decimals) without telling consumers. There was no data contract or schema review.
  5. Kabir converts new rows in silver and reprocesses the affected date partitions, so history is correct again.
  6. Two protections are added: a data contract with the payments team specifying the unit, and a distribution check that stops publishing if the median amount leaves its normal range.

Quality gate 5 steps
Animated example
  1. A nightly batch of 10,030 order rows lands in the lake. Before it reaches analysts, four rules run.
  2. The amount check finds 40 negative values and the uniqueness check finds 30 orders sent twice, a common side effect of retries.
  3. The completeness check finds 25 rows with no city and the validity check finds 15 phones that are not 10-digit Indian mobile numbers. Failing rows are written to a quarantine table, not deleted.

Access control and masking 5 steps
Animated example

  2. An analyst running the same query sees names hidden and phones masked. Column masking policies apply automatically based on the user’s role and the column’s PII tag.

An erasure request and a breach under the DPDP Act

Privacy law turns data engineering choices into deadlines. Watch how a well-governed lake handles a customer’s erasure request, and what must happen when a breach is found.

DPDP in a data platform 5 steps
Animated example
  1. A customer withdraws consent and asks for her data to be erased. Because the platform uses a pseudonymous customer key and a catalog with PII tags, the team can list every place her data lives.
  2. Lake tables in Delta Lake or Iceberg support row-level deletes by key. Old file versions that still contain the row are removed by vacuum or snapshot expiry after the retention window.
  3. Derived ML features and exported copies are deleted too. Backups usually can’t be edited, so they expire on a fixed schedule and are never restored for erased customers. The request and its completion are logged.
  4. Separately, a CRM export bucket is found to be publicly readable with two lakh phone numbers. That is a personal data breach.
  5. The team closes access, investigates scope and cause, and under the DPDP Rules informs the Data Protection Board and affected people without delay, with a detailed report to the Board within 72 hours. Penalties for failing to protect or notify can reach ₹200–250 crore. (Learning summary, not legal advice.)

Write your first data quality checks

A few order rows are checked against rules for validity, completeness and uniqueness. Rows that fail are quarantined with the reason. Then try the challenges: freshness, reconciliation, masking, pseudonymous keys and a publish threshold.

orders = [
    {"id": "O1", "city": "Pune",   "amount": 320},
    {"id": "O2", "city": "",       "amount": 150},
    {"id": "O3", "city": "Thane",  "amount": -40},
    {"id": "O1", "city": "Pune",   "amount": 320},
    {"id": "O4", "city": "Mumbai", "amount": 610},
]
seen, good, quarantine = set(), [], []
for o in orders:
    reasons = []
    if not o["city"]:
        reasons.append("missing city")
    if o["amount"] <= 0:
        reasons.append("amount not positive")
    if o["id"] in seen:
        reasons.append("duplicate id")
    seen.add(o["id"])
    (quarantine if reasons else good).append((o["id"], reasons))

print("good      :", [i for i, _ in good])
print("quarantine:", quarantine)

Run it on your PC: a quality gate with privacy masking

This program creates a batch of 10,030 orders with realistic problems (negative amounts, missing cities, invalid phones, duplicates), runs four quality checks, quarantines failing rows, decides whether to publish, and writes a clean CSV where emails become pseudonymous keys and phones are masked. Save it as quality_guard.py.

Check, quarantine, mask, publish

"""A small data-quality gate and privacy step for a batch of orders.
1) generate a realistic messy batch, 2) run quality checks,
3) quarantine bad rows, 4) mask personal data before it reaches analysts."""
import csv, hashlib, random, re
from collections import Counter

random.seed(11)
CITIES = ["Pune", "Mumbai", "Thane", "Nashik"]
rows = []
for i in range(1, 10_001):
    rows.append({"order_id": f"O{i:05d}", "phone": f"9{random.randint(100000000, 999999999)}",
                 "email": f"user{i}@example.com", "city": random.choice(CITIES),
                 "amount": str(random.randint(80, 900)), "order_date": "2026-10-19"})
# inject the kinds of problems real batches have
for i in random.sample(range(10_000), 40):
    rows[i]["amount"] = "-150"                    # negative amount
for i in random.sample(range(10_000), 25):
    rows[i]["city"] = ""                          # missing city
for i in random.sample(range(10_000), 15):
    rows[i]["phone"] = "12345"                    # invalid phone
rows += [dict(rows[i]) for i in random.sample(range(10_000), 30)]   # duplicates

CHECKS = {
    "amount is positive": lambda r: r["amount"].lstrip("-").isdigit() and int(r["amount"]) > 0,
    "city present": lambda r: r["city"] in CITIES,
    "phone is 10 digits": lambda r: re.fullmatch(r"[6-9]\d{9}", r["phone"]) is not None,
}
seen, good, bad, failures = set(), [], [], Counter()
for r in rows:
    problems = [name for name, ok in CHECKS.items() if not ok(r)]
    if r["order_id"] in seen:
        problems.append("order_id unique")
    seen.add(r["order_id"])
    for p in problems:
        failures[p] += 1
    (bad if problems else good).append(r)

print(f"batch rows      : {len(rows):,}")
for name, n in failures.most_common():
    print(f"  FAIL {name:20}: {n}")
print(f"passed          : {len(good):,}  quarantined: {len(bad)}")
fail_rate = len(bad) / len(rows) * 100
print(f"failure rate    : {fail_rate:.2f}%  ->", "PUBLISH" if fail_rate < 2 else "STOP the pipeline")

SALT = "keep-this-secret"       # in real life: from a secrets manager, never in code
def pseudonym(value):
    return hashlib.sha256((SALT + value).encode()).hexdigest()[:12]
def mask_phone(p):
    return "XXXXXX" + p[-4:]

with open("orders_clean.csv", "w", newline="") as f:
    w = csv.DictWriter(f, fieldnames=["order_id", "customer_key", "phone_masked", "city", "amount", "order_date"])
    w.writeheader()
    for r in good:
        w.writerow({"order_id": r["order_id"], "customer_key": pseudonym(r["email"]), "phone_masked": mask_phone(r["phone"]),
                    "city": r["city"], "amount": r["amount"], "order_date": r["order_date"]})
print("\nwrote orders_clean.csv (no raw email or phone). First row:")
print(open("orders_clean.csv").read().splitlines()[1])

# In a terminal:  python quality_guard.py

Interview and viva questions

What are the dimensions of data quality?

The commonly used dimensions are accuracy (values match reality), completeness (no missing required data), consistency (systems and tables agree), timeliness (data is fresh when needed), validity (values follow formats, types and ranges) and uniqueness (no unintended duplicates). Some frameworks add integrity, relevance and accessibility.

What is the difference between data governance and data management?

Data governance sets the rules, roles and accountability: who owns data, what policies apply, how quality and access are decided. Data management is the day-to-day execution: building pipelines, storing, securing, cleaning and delivering data according to those rules.

How do you implement data quality checks in a pipeline?

Define rules with data owners, then run automated tests after each load: schema, row counts, nulls, ranges, uniqueness, referential integrity, freshness and reconciliation with source totals. Quarantine failing rows, stop publishing above a threshold, alert the owner and track quality metrics over time. Tools include dbt tests, Great Expectations, Soda, Deequ and Delta Live Tables expectations.

What is the DPDP Act and how does it affect data engineers?

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is India's law on processing digital personal data, made operational by the DPDP Rules, 2025 with phased timelines. It requires notice and consent, purpose limitation, data minimisation, reasonable security safeguards, honouring rights such as erasure, breach notification and extra care for children's data. For data engineers this means tagging PII, limiting copies, masking, access controls, retention and deletion workflows across lakes and backups, audit logs and breach-response plans.

How do you delete personal data from a data lake?

Locate all copies using a catalog and a consistent customer key, run row-level deletes on tables stored in formats such as Delta Lake, Apache Iceberg or Hudi, remove old file versions with vacuum or snapshot expiry, delete derived tables, features and exports, and let immutable backups expire on a defined schedule without restoring erased records. Record the request and completion for audit.

Pseudonymous key

Print the first 10 hex characters of SHA-256 of "salt" + "asha@example.com".

Input
email
Expected output
d17065027e
Show solution and explanation 
import hashlib
print(hashlib.sha256(("salt" + "asha@example.com").encode()).hexdigest()[:10])
d17065027e

How it works: The same input always gives the same key, so joins still work.

Retention check

Partitions older than 365 days must be deleted. Given ages [12, 400, 365, 800, 90], print those to delete.

Input
ages in days
Expected output
[400, 800]
Show solution and explanation 
print([a for a in [12, 400, 365, 800, 90] if a > 365])
[400, 800]

How it works: Retention rules limit how long personal data is kept.

P8Business problem

Cost of bad data

Each wrongly refunded order costs Rs 240. Duplicates caused 1,850 wrong refunds last month. Print the loss and the yearly loss.

Input
1,850 × Rs 240
Expected output
Rs 444,000 per month, Rs 5,328,000 per year
Show solution and explanation 
m = 1_850 * 240
print(f"Rs {m:,} per month, Rs {m * 12:,} per year")
Rs 444,000 per month, Rs 5,328,000 per year

How it works: Putting a rupee value on quality issues helps prioritise fixes.

P9Business problem

Quality score

A table passed 46 of 50 checks this week. Print the quality score and whether it meets the 95% target.

Input
46/50
Expected output
92% below target
Show solution and explanation 
s = 46 / 50
print(f"{s:.0%}", "meets target" if s >= 0.95 else "below target")
92% below target

How it works: A simple score per table makes quality visible to owners.

Real-world scenario questions (asked in company interviews)

Quality, security and privacy questions from real projects and interviews. Decide your answer first, then open the model answer.

Data qualityFresher

The dashboard shows 100× revenue

A revenue dashboard suddenly shows a figure about 100 times higher than usual. All pipeline jobs succeeded.

The interviewer is checking: can you debug a data issue systematically?

Show a strong answer
  1. Confirm the symptom and its start date; tell stakeholders you are investigating.
  2. Follow lineage upstream, comparing counts and sums at each layer to find where values change.
  3. Check for schema or unit changes (rupees to paise), duplicates, or join explosions.
  4. Fix the transformation and backfill affected partitions.
  5. Prevent recurrence: range/distribution checks, a data contract with the source team, and alerts.

Avoid: patching the dashboard number by hand.

Security1–3 years

Analysts need customer data, safely

Analysts want access to the customers table, which includes names, phones and addresses.

The interviewer is checking: do you apply least privilege without blocking useful work?

Show a strong answer
  • Classify columns and tag PII in the catalog.
  • Give analysts a view or policy with names removed, phones masked and a pseudonymous customer key for joins.
  • Use row-level filters where teams should see only their region.
  • Keep full access for the few roles that need it, with audit logs and periodic access reviews.
  • Block exports of PII to unmanaged locations.

Avoid: giving everyone full access “just for now”, or refusing access entirely.

Privacy · DPDP1–3 years

Delete one customer from the lake

A customer withdraws consent and asks for erasure. Their data is in raw files, Delta tables, ML features and nightly backups.

The interviewer is checking: do you understand erasure at big data scale?

Show a strong answer

Use the catalog and a pseudonymous key to find all locations. Run row-level deletes on table-format tables (Delta, Iceberg, Hudi), rewrite or expire raw files, vacuum old versions after the retention window, delete derived features and exports, and let immutable backups expire on schedule without restoring erased customers. Record the request and completion. Design ahead: keep PII in few places, separate it from event data, and use keys that make deletion cheap.

Avoid: saying “data lakes are append-only, so we can’t delete”.

Fundamentals · interviewFresher

“What is data governance?”

A common interview and exam question.

The interviewer is checking: can you define it with concrete components and tools?

Show a strong answer

Data governance is the framework of roles, policies and processes that keeps data accurate, understood, secure and used lawfully. Components: ownership and stewardship, a data catalog with business definitions, lineage, classification of sensitive data, quality rules and monitoring, access policies, retention and deletion rules, and compliance with laws such as India’s DPDP Act. Tools include Unity Catalog, Purview, Dataplex, Glue Data Catalog with Lake Formation, DataHub and OpenMetadata.

Avoid: describing governance only as “security”.
