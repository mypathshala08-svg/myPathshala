1 The story

1:10 pm, one data centre goes dark

Before you start: Lessons 1.1 and 1.2 (account with an admin user and budget).

🤔 At 1:10 pm on a weekday, one data centre in Mumbai lost power. TiffinGo’s app kept taking lunch orders. The competitor across the road was down for two hours.

Both ran on AWS Mumbai. TiffinGo had placed its servers in two Availability Zones; the competitor had everything in one. This lesson explains how AWS’s physical world is built — Regions, Availability Zones, data centres, Local Zones, Wavelength Zones, Outposts and edge locations — how to choose a Region, and how failure is contained. On your own account (or in the simulator) you will list Regions, map AZ names to AZ IDs, find the Delhi and Kolkata Local Zones, and measure latency to Regions yourself.

  • Region = where your data lives; AZ = how you survive a data-centre failure.
  • AZ names differ between accounts — AZ IDs do not.
  • Edge locations bring content close to users; they are not where your servers run.

At lunch time one Mumbai data centre lost power. TiffinGo, spread over two Availability Zones, kept taking orders; a competitor in a single AZ was down for two hours.

2 Definition

AWS global infrastructure

Formal definition (write this in exams)

The AWS global infrastructure is the worldwide physical foundation of AWS: geographically separate Regions, each made of multiple isolated, interconnected Availability Zones built from one or more data centres; extended by Local Zones, Wavelength Zones and Outposts; and complemented by edge locations for content delivery and DNS — enabling customers to choose where data resides and to design for high availability and low latency.

In simple words

AWS has data-centre groups (AZs) in many cities (Regions). Put your app in more than one AZ to survive failures, pick the Region for your users and laws, and use edge locations to deliver content fast.

Say it in an interview

30-second answer you can speak:

  • “A Region is a geographic area like Mumbai with at least three isolated Availability Zones; I deploy across AZs for high availability and copy backups to a second Region for disaster recovery.”
  • “Edge locations run CloudFront and Route 53 near users; Local Zones such as Delhi extend Mumbai for single-digit-millisecond latency; Outposts bring AWS on-premises.”

3 The big picture

From the planet to one data centre

New to computers? Start here. Think of AWS as a company with warehouses in many cities. Each city (Region) has several separate buildings (Availability Zones) so that if one building loses power, the others keep working. Small pickup counters (edge locations) near customers hand out popular items quickly.

Jargon buster:

  • Region — a city/area with AWS buildings, e.g. Mumbai
  • Availability Zone — one of several separate buildings in that Region
  • Data centre — the actual building full of computers
  • Latency — the waiting time for data to travel
  • Edge location — a small site near users that keeps copies of content

Type 1 · Region

Definition (say it in an interview): A Region is a separate geographic area (such as ap-south-1, Mumbai) containing multiple isolated Availability Zones; Regions are independent of each other, and data stays in the Region where you put it unless you explicitly copy it elsewhere.

In plain words (no jargon): One of AWS’s geographic areas, such as Mumbai.

Analogy: A city with several independent power stations: you choose which city to live in; the city’s design keeps the lights on.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Latency. Close to users.
  2. Law. Data residency rules.
  3. Services. Is the service there?
  4. Price. Varies by Region.
  5. DR. Pair with a second Region.

Words used above, in simple terms: Region code = ap-south-1, us-east-1… · opt-in Region = newer Regions disabled by default · partition = aws, aws-cn, aws-us-gov — separate worlds

Type 2 · Availability Zone (AZ)

Definition (say it in an interview): An Availability Zone is one or more discrete data centres with redundant power, networking and connectivity in a Region; AZs in a Region are physically separated (many kilometres, but within 100 km) and connected by high-bandwidth, low-latency links, so applications can run across them for high availability.

In plain words (no jargon): An isolated group of data centres inside a Region.

Analogy: Three branches of the same bank in one city, each with its own generator: one branch flooding does not stop the others.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Subnet. Each subnet lives in one AZ.
  2. Spread. Instances in 2–3 AZs.
  3. LB. Distributes across AZs.
  4. Replicate. Multi-AZ DB.
  5. Pay. Inter-AZ data transfer.

Words used above, in simple terms: AZ name = ap-south-1a — mapped per account · AZ ID = aps1-az1 — same physical AZ for everyone · Multi-AZ = deployed across AZs

Type 3 · Data centre

Definition (say it in an interview): A data centre is the physical building housing servers, storage and network equipment; AWS does not disclose locations, customers never visit them, and physical security of the data centre is AWS’s responsibility under the shared responsibility model.

In plain words (no jargon): The physical building where AWS servers run.

Analogy: A bank’s cash vault: you use the money, but you never walk into the vault.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Security. Guards, CCTV, biometrics.
  2. Power. UPS + generators.
  3. Cooling. Redundant.
  4. Disposal. Disks destroyed securely.
  5. Audit. SOC/ISO reports.

Words used above, in simple terms: colocation = renting rack space in someone else’s building · UPS = battery backup · SOC report = independent audit of controls

Type 4 · Local Zone

Definition (say it in an interview): A Local Zone extends a Region into a metro area, placing selected compute, storage and database services close to users for single-digit-millisecond latency; India has Local Zones in Delhi (ap-south-1-del-1a) and Kolkata (ap-south-1-ccu-1a), both with Mumbai as parent Region; you opt in and use them through subnets of your VPC.

In plain words (no jargon): A small extension of a Region in another city.

Analogy: A franchise counter of a Mumbai restaurant in Delhi: a small menu, but much faster for Delhi customers.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Opt in. modify-availability-zone-group.
  2. Subnet. In the Local Zone.
  3. Launch. Supported types only.
  4. Connect. Via parent Region.
  5. Pay. Often higher prices.

Words used above, in simple terms: parent Region = the Region a Local Zone belongs to · zone group = ap-south-1-del-1 · zone type = local-zone

Type 5 · Wavelength Zone

Definition (say it in an interview): A Wavelength Zone embeds AWS compute and storage inside a telecommunications provider’s 5G network, so traffic from mobile devices reaches applications without leaving the operator network — for ultra-low-latency mobile use cases; availability depends on partner operators and cities.

In plain words (no jargon): AWS computers placed inside a 5G mobile network.

Analogy: A food stall inside the railway station instead of outside it: commuters do not even leave the platform.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Partner. Telecom operator.
  2. Opt in. Zone group.
  3. Subnet. In the zone.
  4. Carrier gateway. Connects to mobile network.
  5. App. Low-latency edge part.

Words used above, in simple terms: 5G = fifth-generation mobile network · carrier gateway = routes traffic to/from the operator network · MEC = multi-access edge computing

Type 6 · AWS Outposts

Definition (say it in an interview): AWS Outposts is fully managed AWS infrastructure (racks or servers) installed in a customer’s own data centre or site, running AWS services such as EC2, EBS and RDS locally with the same APIs and tools, connected back to a parent Region — for low latency to on-premises systems, local data processing or data residency.

In plain words (no jargon): AWS servers installed and managed in your own building.

Analogy: Renting a branded vending machine for your office: the company stocks and repairs it, you use it on your floor.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. Order. Console.
  2. Site prep. Power, network, space.
  3. Install. By AWS.
  4. Link. To parent Region.
  5. Operate. AWS maintains hardware.

Words used above, in simple terms: hybrid cloud = on-premises + cloud working together · service link = connection from Outpost to its Region · rack = cabinet of servers

Type 7 · Edge locations (points of presence)

Definition (say it in an interview): Edge locations are sites in many more cities than Regions where AWS runs CloudFront (content delivery), Route 53 (DNS), Global Accelerator and edge security (Shield, WAF); regional edge caches sit between edge locations and the origin to keep more content close to users. Your servers do not run there.

In plain words (no jargon): Small AWS sites near users that cache and deliver content.

Analogy: Local courier pickup counters: the warehouse is in Mumbai, but parcels wait at a counter near you.

How it works internally, step by step

  1. DNS. Route 53 at the edge.
  2. Cache. CloudFront.
  3. Origin. S3 / ALB / EC2.
  4. TTL. How long cached.
  5. Security. WAF, Shield at the edge.

Words used above, in simple terms: PoP = point of presence · origin = where the original content lives · cache hit = served from the edge

5 How it works, step by step

An Availability Zone fails — what happens

TiffinGo’s outage, step by step.

Animated

An Availability Zone fails — watch what happens

TiffinGo’s 1:10 pm outage, as it happens inside Mumbai. Blue dots are customer requests; green dots are database copies.

AZ failure 6 steps
Watch what happens
  1. Step 1 · Normal lunch hour — the load balancer spreads orders over servers in AZ a and AZ b; every database write is copied synchronously from the primary (AZ b) to the standby (AZ a).
  2. Step 2 · 1:10 pm — AZ b loses power — its servers and the database primary stop. AZ a and AZ c are separate buildings with separate power, so they keep running.
  3. Step 3 · Health checks notice — the load balancer probes every target every few seconds; after consecutive failures AZ b’s servers are marked unhealthy and get no new orders.
  4. Step 4 · All orders flow to AZ a — capacity is halved, CPU climbs to about 88% — this is why you size for losing one AZ (N+1).
  5. Step 5 · Auto Scaling heals — the Auto Scaling group sees missing capacity and launches new servers in the healthy AZ c within minutes; CPU drops back.
  6. Step 6 · Database fails over — RDS promotes the standby in AZ a to primary and repoints its DNS name in about 1–2 minutes; because copies were synchronous, no order is lost.
6 Explained part by part

Six parts of designing with global infrastructure

Region choice

What it is: Latency, law, services, price.

Why it exists: The most permanent decision.

Where it is used: Write it in the design doc.

Multi-AZ design

What it is: Every tier in 2–3 AZs.

Why it exists: Survives a data-centre failure.

Where it is used: Subnets per AZ, LB, Multi-AZ DB.

Multi-Region (DR)

What it is: Second Region with data copies.

Why it exists: Survives a Region-wide event.

Where it is used: Backups, replication, Route 53 failover.

Edge delivery

What it is: CloudFront, Route 53.

Why it exists: Speed for distant users.

Where it is used: Cache static content.

Extensions

What it is: Local Zones, Wavelength, Outposts.

Why it exists: Special latency/residency needs.

Where it is used: Opt in deliberately.

Global services

What it is: IAM, Route 53, CloudFront, Organizations.

Why it exists: Not tied to one Region.

Where it is used: Billing data in us-east-1.

Myth vs reality: things many websites still say

  • Myth: AWS never fails.
    Reality: AZs can fail; design decides whether you survive.
    Proof: Story.
  • Myth: ap-south-1a is the same everywhere.
    Reality: Names are mapped per account; IDs are not.
    Proof: Type 2.
  • Myth: More Regions is always better.
    Reality: Multi-AZ + cross-Region backups suit most.

9 Under the hood

AZ ID mapping, network latency, partitions, global vs regional, data-transfer paths

What sits behind the Region selector — the details architects and SREs rely on.

1 · AZ names are shuffled per account; AZ IDs are not

When you see ap-south-1a, AWS has mapped that name to one physical AZ for your account. Another account’s ap-south-1a may be a different AZ. The AZ ID (aps1-az1, aps1-az2, aps1-az3) is the same for everyone. Use IDs when you share subnets (VPC sharing), coordinate a migration with a vendor, or read an AWS Health event that names an AZ.

aws ec2 describe-availability-zones --region ap-south-1 --query 'AvailabilityZones[].{Name:ZoneName,Id:ZoneId}' --output table

2 · How far apart and how fast

AWS states AZs in a Region are separated by a meaningful distance — many kilometres — yet all within 100 km, linked by redundant, dedicated fibre with low latency (typically low single-digit milliseconds round trip), fast enough for synchronous replication (RDS Multi-AZ, EBS-backed services, Aurora storage across 3 AZs). Between Regions, latency follows distance: light in fibre covers roughly 200 km per millisecond, so Mumbai → N. Virginia (~13,000 km) can never be faster than about 65 ms one way, and real paths are longer.

3 · Partitions: worlds that do not share accounts

Regions are grouped into partitions: aws (commercial Regions, including India), aws-cn (China, operated by local partners, separate accounts), aws-us-gov (GovCloud) and the AWS European Sovereign Cloud. An IAM user in one partition cannot be used in another — the partition is the first field of every ARN: arn:aws:s3:::bucket.

4 · Global, regional and zonal — and where the control plane lives

ScopeExamplesFailure blast radius
GlobalIAM, Route 53, CloudFront, Organizationscontrol plane hosted in one Region (often us-east-1); data plane global and highly available
RegionalS3, DynamoDB, Lambda, SQS, VPCservice is spread across the Region’s AZs for you
ZonalEC2 instance, EBS volume, subnet, NAT gatewayyou must spread across AZs yourself

Lesson: regional managed services (S3, DynamoDB, Lambda) give you multi-AZ for free; zonal resources (EC2, EBS) make multi-AZ your job.

10 By role

How developers, data, ML, DevOps and security people use it

Every role meets global infrastructure, but at a different layer.

Software developer (backend)

Writes code that must survive an AZ failure.

Uses: SDK Region config (AWS_REGION), multi-AZ RDS endpoints, retries with backoff, health-check endpoints (/health).

A real task: Make the order API stateless so any instance in any AZ can serve any request; keep sessions in ElastiCache/DynamoDB, not on the server.

Skill to build: Statelessness, idempotent retries, timeouts.

App / mobile developer

Wants fast screens for users far away.

Uses: CloudFront in front of S3/API Gateway, Route 53 latency routing, Amplify hosting.

A real task: Dubai users load the menu photos from a nearby edge; API calls still go to Mumbai.

Skill to build: Caching headers (Cache-Control), CDN invalidations.

Data analyst

Must know where data lives before querying or sharing it.

Uses: Athena/QuickSight in the same Region as the data; cross-Region queries cost transfer.

A real task: A Mumbai data lake queried from a Singapore QuickSight account → slow and charged; move the dashboard to ap-south-1.

Skill to build: Data locality and residency.

Data engineer

Designs where pipelines run and how data is replicated.

Uses: S3 Cross-Region Replication, Glue/EMR in the data’s Region, DynamoDB global tables.

A real task: Replicate the curated zone to Hyderabad for DR; keep raw data only in Mumbai to save cost.

Skill to build: Replication, partition placement, transfer cost.

ML / AI engineer

GPU and model availability differ by Region.

Uses: Check SageMaker instance types and Bedrock model availability per Region; training data in the same Region.

A real task: A foundation model is not yet in Mumbai — use cross-Region inference only if data rules allow, otherwise wait or self-host.

Skill to build: Region availability checks, data residency for training data.

DevOps / cloud engineer

Implements the resilience ladder.

Uses: Subnets in every AZ, Auto Scaling across AZs, Route 53 failover, AWS Backup cross-Region copies, AWS Health events.

A real task: Game day: simulate an AZ failure (AWS Fault Injection Service) and prove the app survives.

Skill to build: Multi-AZ by default, DR runbooks, chaos testing.

Security engineer

Enforces “data stays in India”.

Uses: SCP denying actions outside approved Regions (aws:RequestedRegion), Config rules, CloudTrail in all Regions.

A real task: Block any resource creation outside ap-south-1/ap-south-2 for the bank’s accounts.

Skill to build: Region guard-rails, multi-Region logging.

Global infrastructure design checklist

  • ☐ Region chosen with the four-question test and recorded.
  • ☐ Every tier in at least two AZs (subnets, instances, database).
  • ☐ Capacity sized to survive losing one AZ (N+1).
  • ☐ Backups copied to a second Region (e.g. ap-south-2).
  • ☐ Static content behind CloudFront.
  • ☐ SCP or policy limits Regions where resources may be created.
  • ☐ You know your AZ IDs.

14 Hands-on

Do it yourself, step by step

These labs run on your own AWS account — we cannot run them for you. Every step shows the UI way (console clicks) and the CLI way (commands for AWS CloudShell or your terminal), plus what you should see. Work as an IAM/Identity Center user, never root; use the Mumbai Region (ap-south-1); stay inside Free Tier/credits; and always do the Clean up step at the end. This lab only reads information (and optionally opts in to a Local Zone, which is free). Cost: zero.

  1. 0 · Get ready: signed in, budget, Region, CloudShell

    UI way — AWS Management Console

    1. Sign in as your admin user (not root) — Lesson 1.2 shows how to create it.
    2. Billing and Cost Management → Budgets → check a monthly cost budget with e-mail alerts exists (e.g. $5).
    3. Top-right Region menu → Asia Pacific (Mumbai).
    4. Click the CloudShell icon (>_) in the top bar — the AWS CLI is already signed in there.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    aws sts get-caller-identity --query Arn --output text   # must NOT end in :root
aws configure set region ap-south-1
aws configure list | grep region

    You should see: an ARN ending in :user/… or :assumed-role/… and region ap-south-1.

    CloudShell is free, runs in your browser and is already signed in as the user you logged in with.

  2. 1 · List Regions and see which are enabled

    UI way — AWS Management Console

    1. EC2 → EC2 Global View → Region explorer: enabled Regions and resource counts.
    2. Top-right Region menu: Regions shown in grey need opting in (Account → AWS Regions).

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    aws ec2 describe-regions --all-regions --query 'Regions[].[RegionName,OptInStatus]' --output table
aws ec2 describe-regions --all-regions --query 'length(Regions)'

    You should see: a table of every Region with opt-in-not-required, opted-in or not-opted-in, and the total count. Hyderabad (ap-south-2) is an opt-in Region.

    Regions launched after March 2019 are disabled until you opt in — a safety feature so nothing runs in a Region you did not choose.

  3. 2 · Map AZ names to AZ IDs, and find the Local Zones

    UI way — AWS Management Console

    1. EC2 → Settings → Zones: see AZ names, AZ IDs and Local Zones with their opt-in status.
    2. Optional (free): opt in to the Delhi Local Zone group.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    aws ec2 describe-availability-zones --region ap-south-1 \
  --query 'AvailabilityZones[].[ZoneName,ZoneId,ZoneType,State]' --output table
aws ec2 describe-availability-zones --region ap-south-1 --all-availability-zones \
  --filters Name=zone-type,Values=local-zone --query 'AvailabilityZones[].[ZoneName,ZoneId,OptInStatus,ParentZoneId]' --output table
# optional, free: enable the Delhi Local Zone group
aws ec2 modify-availability-zone-group --region ap-south-1 --group-name ap-south-1-del-1 --opt-in-status opted-in

    You should see: three AZs (ap-south-1a/b/c) with IDs like aps1-az1, then ap-south-1-del-1a (aps1-del1-az1) and ap-south-1-ccu-1a (aps1-ccu1-az1). Compare AZ IDs with a classmate: the same name may map to a different ID.

    AWS shuffles AZ names per account so that everybody choosing “a” does not crowd one building. AZ IDs are the real, shared identifiers.

  4. 3 · Measure latency to Regions yourself

    UI way — AWS Management Console

    1. Open CloudShell (Mumbai). Run the loop below, then run it again from your laptop terminal and compare.

    CLI way — the same thing with commands

    for r in ap-south-1 ap-south-2 ap-southeast-1 me-central-1 eu-west-1 us-east-1; do
  printf '%-15s ' $r
  curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{time_connect}s\n' https://ec2.$r.amazonaws.com/
done

    You should see: TCP connect times in seconds: a few milliseconds to ap-south-1, tens of ms to Singapore and the Gulf, 100+ ms to Ireland and N. Virginia. From your laptop, add your ISP’s delay.

    Light in fibre needs about 5 ms per 1,000 km one way — distance is the floor no technology removes. That is why Region choice and edge caching matter.

Clean up and cost check

Nothing to delete. If you opted in to the Delhi Local Zone and want to undo it: aws ec2 modify-availability-zone-group --region ap-south-1 --group-name ap-south-1-del-1 --opt-in-status not-opted-in (allowed only when nothing is running there).

15 Mistakes to avoid

Common mistakes, why they happen, how to fix them

Mistake or symptomWhy it happensHow to fix or avoid
Calling one instance “highly available”.It is running, so it feels available.At least two instances in two AZs behind a load balancer.
Assuming ap-south-1a is the same building for everyone.Same name.Use AZ IDs (aps1-az1) across accounts.
Choosing us-east-1 by default.Tutorials use it.Choose by users and law — usually ap-south-1 for India.
Thinking edge locations run servers.They are “AWS sites”.They cache content and run DNS/edge functions only.
Multi-Region for everything.More = safer.Multi-AZ + cross-Region backups is enough for most; active-active costs 2×+.
Ignoring inter-AZ data cost.Same Region feels free.Inter-AZ traffic is charged per GB in each direction; keep chatty parts together or cache.

Quick revision

Lesson 1.3 in one screen

  • Region = where data lives; choose by law, latency, services, price.
  • Multi-AZ = survive a data-centre failure; multi-Region = DR.
  • AZ names are per account; AZ IDs are shared.

How many Regions does AWS have?

39 Regions and 124 AZs as of Nov 2026 — check the live page; it grows.

Is Hyderabad better than Mumbai?

Mumbai has more services; Hyderabad is ideal as a second Region for DR in India.

Do Local Zones cost extra to enable?

No; you pay for resources you launch there (often at higher prices).
