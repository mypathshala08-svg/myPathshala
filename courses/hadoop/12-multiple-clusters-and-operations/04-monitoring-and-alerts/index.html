Monitoring and alerts

2 Definition

Monitoring and alerting for Hadoop

Formal definition (write this in exams)

Monitoring is the continuous collection of metrics, logs and health checks from every daemon and host of a cluster, stored as time series and shown on dashboards. Alerting evaluates rules over those metrics (a condition, a threshold and a duration) and notifies people or systems when a rule fires, with a severity and a runbook. Hadoop daemons expose metrics through the metrics2 framework, readable over HTTP as JSON at /jmx (NameNode, DataNode, ResourceManager, NodeManager, JobHistory Server) and through the YARN REST API; they can also push to sinks. Collectors such as Prometheus (with the JMX exporter), Ambari Metrics or Cloudera Manager scrape these endpoints, and alert managers route notifications to on-call tools. Good alerting targets symptoms that threaten data or service-level objectives (SLOs), uses durations to suppress noise, and accounts for the delay with which each metric is updated.

In simple words

Every Hadoop daemon publishes numbers about itself on a web page. A collector reads them every few seconds, a rule says “if this number is bad for this long, tell someone”, and the message goes to the person on call with a note on what to do.

Monitoring at three levels

Pick your level. New to this? Start at Beginner and climb.

Beginner A hospital ward

  • Every patient is connected to a monitor that shows heart rate and oxygen: those are metrics.
  • The machine beeps when oxygen stays below 90% for a while, not for one bad reading: that is an alert rule with a duration.
  • A nurse at the desk sees all beds on one screen: the dashboard. The doctor on call is phoned only for serious beeps: severity.
  • A beeping machine with a loose sensor is a false positive. Too many of those and nurses stop listening.

Intermediate What to watch on a Hadoop cluster

  • HDFS: capacity used %, live/dead/stale DataNodes, missing and corrupt blocks (data at risk), under-replicated blocks (safety margin shrinking), safe mode, NameNode heap and GC pauses, RPC queue time, time since last checkpoint.
  • YARN: active/unhealthy/lost NodeManagers, pending applications, queue usage vs capacity, ApplicationMaster failures, containers killed for memory.
  • Jobs: did the nightly job finish before its deadline? Job duration trends, failed attempts.
  • Hosts: disk full or failing, memory, swap, network errors, clock drift (Kerberos breaks above 5 minutes).

Advanced Designing alerts people trust

  • Alert on symptoms that threaten data or an SLO (missing blocks, job late, safe mode), not on every cause; causes go on dashboards.
  • Use durations (“for 5 minutes”) and severities; every CRIT has a runbook and an owner.
  • Know each metric's lag: stale nodes were recounted every 5 minutes in our lab, and JMX counters such as FilesTotal update with a delay (Lesson 7.5).
  • Fix the source of noise: our 7 staging-file blocks need mapreduce.client.submit.file.replication ≤ number of DataNodes, or regular cleanup, not a muted alert.
  • Add black-box checks: a canary job that writes, reads and runs a tiny MapReduce job proves the whole path works, which no single metric can.

Exam tip: For “How do you monitor a Hadoop cluster?”, name the source (JMX/metrics2 over HTTP, YARN REST), a collector (Prometheus/Ambari/Cloudera Manager), five key metrics (missing blocks, dead DataNodes, capacity %, safe mode, unhealthy NodeManagers) and two alert-design rules (duration, severity + runbook).

On call during a sale 6 steps
Animated example
  1. 20:00: the sale opens and traffic jumps sixfold. All dashboards are green and the runbook is ready.
  2. 21:40: a WARN fires at 81% disk use. The runbook says to clear expired temporary data; usage drops to 74%.
  3. 23:10: three DataNodes in one rack go stale. The rule waits five minutes to be sure, then pages the network team.
  4. 23:40: under-replicated blocks jump, which is expected after a rack problem. Missing blocks stay at zero, so it is a WARN.

  6. 06:00: the sale report is on time. The morning review adds a disk-growth forecast alert. (Illustrative scenario.)

The best first alert for any cluster is MissingBlocks > 0. It almost never fires falsely, and when it fires, data is unreadable right now.

4 Step by step

Build a monitor, then break the cluster

Our monitor is about 60 lines of Python (monitor.py, in “Run it on your PC”). It runs on a lab8 worker that can reach both clusters: prod (the Module 8 lab) and dr (the federation lab from Lesson 7.2).

  1. Read the metrics

    curl -s 'http://namenode:9870/jmx?qry=Hadoop:service=NameNode,name=FSNamesystemState'
curl -s http://resourcemanager:8088/ws/v1/cluster/metrics
    10:21:11 prod live=3 dead=0 stale=0 used=0.6 under=0 missing=0 files=1658 safemode=False nodes=3 unhealthy=0 lost=0 pending=0 running=0 mem=0 10:21:11 dr live=3 dead=0 stale=0 used=0.1 under=0 missing=0 files=15 safemode=False
    The baseline. Everything a rule needs is in two JSON documents per cluster: no agents, no extra software.

  2. Stop a DataNode and wait for the alert

    docker exec lab8-worker2-1 hdfs --daemon stop datanode
    (the alert appeared 296 s after the DataNode stopped) 10:26:08 prod live=3 dead=0 stale=1 used=0.6 under=0 ... 10:26:08 ALERT WARN prod: 1 stale DataNode(s) (no heartbeat for 30 s)
    Stale after 30 s, but the stale count is refreshed on the NameNode's heartbeat check (every 5 minutes by default), so our alert came almost 5 minutes late. Note that live still says 3: the node is declared dead only after 10 min 30 s.

  3. Ask for more replicas than the cluster can give

    hdfs dfs -setrep 4 /user/hadoop/data/menu.csv
    Replication 4 set: /user/hadoop/data/menu.csv 10:26:16 ALERT WARN prod: 1 under-replicated blocks
    This one shows up at the next check: the block's target is 4 copies and only 3 DataNodes exist (2 alive).

  4. Safe mode on the DR cluster

    docker exec lab72-nn-canteen-1 hdfs dfsadmin -safemode enter
    Safe mode is ON 10:26:17 dr live=3 ... safemode=True 10:26:17 ALERT CRIT dr: NameNode in safe mode (read-only)
    CRIT, because every write to that cluster now fails (Lesson 5.6). The same rule covers both clusters.

  5. Fill YARN with two big jobs

    10:26:52 prod live=3 dead=0 stale=1 used=0.6 under=8 missing=0 files=1675 ... pending=1 running=1 mem=50 10:26:52 ALERT WARN prod: 8 under-replicated blocks 10:26:52 ALERT WARN prod: 1 app(s) waiting to start (cluster memory 50% used)
    Two surprises. The second job waited while half the memory was free: the queue's ApplicationMaster limit stopped it (Lesson 12.6). And under-replicated blocks jumped from 1 to 8: each job writes its job.jar and job.split with replication 10.

  6. Fix everything and look again

    (stale cleared 246 s after the restart) 10:31:08 prod live=3 dead=0 stale=0 used=0.6 under=7 missing=0 files=1680 ... 10:31:08 ALERT WARN prod: 7 under-replicated blocks
    The jobs were killed, safe mode left, replication reset and the DataNode restarted. Seven blocks still alert: staging files left by the killed jobs, wanting 10 copies forever. Before we cleaned up at the start, the same cause showed 38. Fix the cause (mapreduce.client.submit.file.replication=3, clean old staging folders), don't mute the alert.

Never silence an alert you don't understand. Our “7 under-replicated blocks” was harmless, but the same alert on a real cluster can mean a disk is slowly failing.

6 Real-world use

Monitoring at work

1. Bank data lake: data-loss alerts first

A bank's platform team pages only for missing or corrupt blocks, safe mode and the regulatory report SLA; everything else goes to a daytime queue so the on-call engineer trusts every page.

NameNode JMX→CRIT rules→Pager

2. Telecom: capacity forecasting

Call-detail records grow every month. A forecast alert (“disk full in 45 days at this rate”) gives procurement time to buy nodes, instead of a 90% alert at midnight.

Capacity history→Linear forecast→Ticket, not page

3. E-commerce: canary jobs

A tiny job writes a file, reads it back and runs a word count every 10 minutes. If it fails or slows down, the team knows users are affected even when every metric is green.

Scheduler→Canary job→Duration alert

4. Government data platform: one view of many clusters

Departments run separate clusters (Lesson 12.1); a central team scrapes all of them into one Prometheus with a cluster label, like our monitor watching prod and dr together.

Many clusters→One collector→Fleet dashboard

5. Hospital analytics: log-based alerts

Some problems show up only in logs: a NameNode “JvmPauseMonitor: Detected pause” line, or OutOfMemoryError in a nightly job. A log shipper counts such patterns and alerts on them.

Daemon logs→Pattern counter→Alert

Simplified; organisations differ in tools and policies.

7 Run the code

An alert rule engine over real snapshots

These snapshots are the prod numbers our monitor printed during the lab, one per check. The engine applies the same rules as monitor.py, plus one improvement: a rule can require its condition to hold for several checks in a row before it fires.

snapshots = [   # prod, from tx124 (time, stale DataNodes, under-replicated blocks, pending apps)
    ("10:21:11", 0, 0, 0), ("10:26:08", 1, 0, 0), ("10:26:16", 1, 1, 0),
    ("10:26:17", 1, 1, 0), ("10:26:52", 1, 8, 1), ("10:31:08", 0, 7, 0),
]
rules = [   # (name, test, checks in a row needed, severity)
    ("stale DataNode", lambda s: s[1] > 0, 1, "WARN"),
    ("under-replicated", lambda s: s[2] > 0, 1, "WARN"),
    ("app waiting", lambda s: s[3] > 0, 1, "WARN"),
]
streak = {name: 0 for name, _, _, _ in rules}
for snap in snapshots:
    fired = []
    for name, test, need, sev in rules:
        streak[name] = streak[name] + 1 if test(snap) else 0
        if streak[name] >= need:
            fired.append(f"{sev} {name}")
    print(snap[0], fired or "ok")

Run it on your PC: a monitor for two clusters, and four incidents

You need the Module 8 lab (prod) and the Lesson 7.2 federation lab (dr) running, with the lab8 workers joined to the dr network as in Lesson 12.2 (join_clusters.sh). monitor.py runs on lab8-worker1-1 because that machine can reach both clusters. The lab script stops a DataNode, changes replication, enters safe mode, starts two big jobs, then undoes everything. It takes about 15 minutes, mostly waiting for the stale count to refresh.

The monitor

# monitor.py: a tiny fleet monitor. Reads the JMX/REST endpoints of every NameNode and ResourceManager,
# applies alert rules, prints one line per check. Usage: python3 monitor.py [rounds] [seconds_between]
import json, sys, time, urllib.request

CLUSTERS = {                      # name: (NameNode web, ResourceManager web or None)
    "prod": ("http://namenode:9870", "http://resourcemanager:8088"),
    "dr":   ("http://nn-canteen:9870", None),
}

def get(url):
    with urllib.request.urlopen(url, timeout=5) as r:
        return json.load(r)

def nn_metrics(base):
    beans = get(base + "/jmx?qry=Hadoop:service=NameNode,name=*")["beans"]
    m = {}
    for b in beans:
        m.update(b)
    return m

def check(cluster, nn, rm):
    alerts, info = [], {}
    try:
        m = nn_metrics(nn)
    except Exception as e:
        return [f"CRIT {cluster}: NameNode not answering ({type(e).__name__})"], info
    used = 100.0 * m["CapacityUsed"] / max(m["CapacityTotal"], 1)
    info.update(live=m["NumLiveDataNodes"], dead=m["NumDeadDataNodes"], stale=m["NumStaleDataNodes"],
                used=round(used, 1), under=m["UnderReplicatedBlocks"], missing=m["MissingBlocks"],
                files=m["FilesTotal"], safemode=bool(m.get("Safemode")))
    if info["missing"] > 0:  alerts.append(f"CRIT {cluster}: {info['missing']} missing blocks")
    if info["dead"] > 0:     alerts.append(f"CRIT {cluster}: {info['dead']} dead DataNode(s)")
    if info["stale"] > 0:    alerts.append(f"WARN {cluster}: {info['stale']} stale DataNode(s) (no heartbeat for 30 s)")
    if info["under"] > 0:    alerts.append(f"WARN {cluster}: {info['under']} under-replicated blocks")
    if info["safemode"]:     alerts.append(f"CRIT {cluster}: NameNode in safe mode (read-only)")
    if used > 80:            alerts.append(f"WARN {cluster}: HDFS {used:.0f}% full")
    if rm:
        c = get(rm + "/ws/v1/cluster/metrics")["clusterMetrics"]
        mem = 100.0 * c["allocatedMB"] / max(c["totalMB"], 1)
        info.update(nodes=c["activeNodes"], unhealthy=c["unhealthyNodes"], lost=c["lostNodes"],
                    pending=c["appsPending"], running=c["appsRunning"], mem=round(mem))
        if c["unhealthyNodes"] or c["lostNodes"]:
            alerts.append(f"CRIT {cluster}: {c['unhealthyNodes']} unhealthy / {c['lostNodes']} lost NodeManagers")
        if c["appsPending"] > 0:
            alerts.append(f"WARN {cluster}: {c['appsPending']} app(s) waiting to start (cluster memory {mem:.0f}% used)")
    return alerts, info

rounds = int(sys.argv[1]) if len(sys.argv) > 1 else 1
pause = int(sys.argv[2]) if len(sys.argv) > 2 else 10
for r in range(rounds):
    stamp = time.strftime("%H:%M:%S")
    for name, (nn, rm) in CLUSTERS.items():
        alerts, info = check(name, nn, rm)
        print(f"{stamp} {name:4} " + " ".join(f"{k}={v}" for k, v in info.items()))
        for a in alerts:
            print(f"{stamp}   ALERT {a}")
    if r < rounds - 1:
        time.sleep(pause)

8 Line by line

A “for 3 checks” rule step by step

Watch the streak counter grow and reset. The rule fires only when the condition held for 3 checks in a row, so one bad reading is ignored.

Worked examples: real problems, solved and traced in memory

Four monitoring problems from different domains: designing thresholds, measuring alert noise, forecasting capacity and checking an SLA with a canary.

Example 1BeginnerThresholds · HDFS

WARN and CRIT for disk use at a hospital

Problem statement

A hospital records cluster has 600 TB of HDFS. Data grows 2 TB a day. The team wants a WARN with at least 2 weeks to react and a CRIT with at least 3 days. What usage percentages should the thresholds be?

Input
600 TB · +2 TB/day · WARN 14 days · CRIT 3 days
Expected output
WARN at 95.3% used (14 days of growth left)
CRIT at 99.0% used (3 days of growth left)

Approach

  1. Space needed for N days = growth × N.
  2. Threshold = (capacity − that space) / capacity.
  3. Round down to a simple number.

Quick revision

Lesson 12.4 in one screen

  • Every daemon exposes metrics at /jmx (JSON); YARN also at /ws/v1/cluster/metrics. Collectors: Prometheus + JMX exporter, Ambari, Cloudera Manager; dashboards in Grafana.
  • HDFS signals: missing/corrupt blocks, dead/stale DataNodes, under-replicated blocks, capacity %, safe mode, NameNode heap/GC, RPC queue time, checkpoint age.
  • YARN signals: unhealthy/lost NodeManagers, pending apps, queue usage, AM failures; plus job SLAs and host health.
  • Lab: stale alert after 296 s (30 s + 5-minute recount); replication 4 → 1 under-replicated; safe mode → CRIT; job waiting at 50% memory (AM limit).
  • False alert: staging files with replication 10 left 7 under-replicated blocks forever. Fix the cause, not the alert.
  • Good alerts: symptom-based, WARN vs CRIT, durations, runbook and owner; know each metric's lag.
  • Add black-box canary jobs and forecast alerts for capacity.

Interview and viva questions

How do you monitor a Hadoop cluster?

Collect the metrics every daemon publishes (JMX over HTTP at /jmx, the YARN REST API, metrics2 sinks) with a collector such as Prometheus with the JMX exporter, Ambari Metrics or Cloudera Manager, plus host metrics and logs. Show them on dashboards, and alert on rules such as missing blocks, dead DataNodes, capacity above a threshold, safe mode, unhealthy NodeManagers and late jobs, each with a severity, a duration and a runbook.

What is the difference between a stale and a dead DataNode?

A DataNode is stale when the NameNode has not received a heartbeat for 30 seconds (dfs.namenode.stale.datanode.interval); clients avoid it for reads and writes. It is dead after about 10 minutes 30 seconds without heartbeats, and only then does the NameNode start re-replicating its blocks. In our lab the stale count metric itself was refreshed every 5 minutes, so the alert came after 296 seconds.

What is the difference between under-replicated and missing blocks?

An under-replicated block has fewer copies than its target but at least one, so data is still readable and HDFS will copy it again. A missing block has no live copy at all; reads fail until a DataNode holding it comes back. Missing blocks should page someone immediately; under-replicated blocks are usually a warning.

How do you avoid alert fatigue?

Alert only on symptoms that need a human, use durations so short blips do not fire, separate WARN (daytime ticket) from CRIT (page), attach a runbook to every alert, review noisy alerts weekly and fix their cause, and send everything else to dashboards. Our staging-file alert is an example: fix mapreduce.client.submit.file.replication instead of muting it.

What is a canary job?

A small synthetic job run on a schedule, for example every 10 minutes, that writes a file, reads it back and runs a tiny MapReduce or Spark job. Its success and duration are monitored. It detects problems users would feel, such as a full queue or slow storage, even when every individual metric looks healthy.

Under or missing?

Classify each block by its live replicas: 0 = missing, below target = under-replicated, else ok.

Input
target 3; live 3, 2, 0, 1
Expected output
3 ok
2 under-replicated
0 missing

P6Medium

Dedupe alerts

The same alert fired at every check. Print it only when it starts and when it clears.

Input
a list of states
Expected output

check 4: RESOLVED
check 5: FIRING
Show solution and explanation 
states = [False, True, True, True, False, True]

for i, s in enumerate(states):

        print(f"check {i}:", "FIRING" if s else "RESOLVED")

P8Advanced

Multi-cluster rules

Apply the same rules to every cluster in a dict and print alerts with the cluster name, like monitor.py.

Input
prod and dr snapshots
Expected output
WARN prod 1 stale DataNode(s)
CRIT dr safe mode
Show solution and explanation 
fleet = {"prod": {"missing": 0, "safemode": False, "stale": 1},
         "dr": {"missing": 0, "safemode": True, "stale": 0}}
for name, m in fleet.items():
    if m["missing"]: print("CRIT", name, "missing blocks")
    if m["safemode"]: print("CRIT", name, "safe mode")
    if m["stale"]: print("WARN", name, m["stale"], "stale DataNode(s)")

How it works: One rule set, many clusters: label every metric with its cluster (Lesson 12.1).

Real-world scenario questions (asked in company interviews)

Monitoring questions are about judgement: what deserves a page, and what you do when one arrives.

Basics · metricsFresher

“Name five metrics you would alert on for HDFS”

A standard question for Hadoop admin and data engineer roles.

The interviewer is checking: that you know which metrics mean data risk.

Show a strong answer
  • Missing and corrupt blocks (page immediately), dead DataNodes, capacity used %, safe mode, under-replicated blocks trend.
  • NameNode heap and GC pauses, RPC queue time, time since the last checkpoint.
  • Read them from the NameNode /jmx endpoint with a collector such as Prometheus.
Operations · noiseExperienced

The under-replicated-blocks alert fires every night and nobody looks any more

You join a team where on-call engineers mute this alert.

The interviewer is checking: root-cause thinking over muting.

Show a strong answer
  • Find which files are under-replicated with hdfs fsck / | grep -i "under replicated"; often they are job staging files with replication 10 on a small cluster.
  • Fix the cause: mapreduce.client.submit.file.replication ≤ DataNodes, clean old staging folders.
  • Change the rule to “growing for 30 minutes” and keep missing blocks as the page.
Incident · latencyExperienced

All metrics are green but analysts say jobs are slow

Dashboards show healthy nodes and 60% disk use.

The interviewer is checking: black-box monitoring and queue awareness.

Show a strong answer
  • Check pending applications and queue usage: a full queue or AM limit keeps jobs in ACCEPTED while memory looks free.
  • Check NameNode RPC queue time and GC pauses, which slow every client.
  • Add a canary job with a duration alert so this is caught before users report it.
Design · fleetArchitect

Design monitoring for 12 clusters in three regions

Each business unit ran its own scripts until now.

The interviewer is checking: standardisation and ownership.

Show a strong answer
  • One Prometheus per region scraping all daemons via the JMX exporter, every metric labelled with cluster and role, central Grafana and Alertmanager.
  • One shared rule set with severities and runbooks; routing by cluster owner; SLOs for key pipelines with canaries.
  • Capacity forecasts and a weekly noise review; test alerts with planned drills like the ones in this lesson.
