1 The story

Four canteens, 130 files and no answer

Kadak Canteens runs four college canteens in Kharghar, Vashi, Panvel and Nerul. Every evening each till (POS machine) saves a CSV file of the day's orders, and every night the UPI gateway sends a file of payments. Mrs. Shinde, the owner, hands Kabir a pen drive with all of September and a question her accountant could not answer in four days of Excel: "How much did each canteen really earn, what sells, when are we busiest, and did all the UPI money reach my account?"

Kabir

Fifty megabytes? My laptop can open that in Excel. Why would I need Hadoop?

Arjun Sir

Fair question, and you should ask it in every project. Today it is one month and four canteens. Mrs. Shinde wants forty canteens and three years of history next year: that is thousands of files a month and gigabytes a day. We build the pipeline now, on a size we can check by hand, and it scales by adding nodes.

Riya

Then the first job is to write code?

Arjun Sir

No. The first job is to understand the problem and the data: what exactly is asked, what is wrong with the files, and how we will prove our answer is right. That is today's lesson. Tomorrow we build.

Kabir

Arjun Sir

For this capstone we do. Our generator writes truth.json, the correct totals, alongside the messy files. In a company the same role is played by a trusted source: a bank statement, the finance ledger, the source database's row counts.

2 Definition

A capstone: one real problem, end to end

Formal definition (write this in exams)

A capstone project applies the whole course to one realistic problem. In data engineering it starts with requirements (the business questions and the deliverables), a source inventory (each dataset's format, frequency, volume, keys and owner), data profiling (measuring the actual content: row counts, distinct keys, nulls, invalid values, duplicates and late records) and a set of data quality rules that say how each problem is handled. It ends with acceptance criteria: measurable checks, ideally reconciled against an independent source of truth, that the delivered tables must pass.

In simple words

Before building anything, write down what the business wants, look hard at the files you were given, list everything that is wrong with them and decide what to do about each problem, and agree on the numbers that will prove you got it right.

Words you'll meet in this lesson

RequirementA question the business needs answered, e.g. revenue per canteen per day.
Source inventoryA list of every input: format, size, how often it arrives, key, owner.
ProfilingMeasuring the data as it really is before trusting it.
GrainWhat one row means: here, one order (not one item, not one day).
Business keyThe column that identifies a row: order_id.
Late recordA row for 12 September that arrives in the file of 14 September.
QuarantineA place for rows that cannot be parsed, kept for inspection, never silently dropped.
Acceptance testA check with an expected number; the pipeline is done when all pass.

The capstone at three levels

Pick your level. New to this? Start at Beginner and climb.

Beginner What we are building

  • A small business has messy daily files from four shops and a payments company.
  • We load them into Hadoop, clean them, and produce four tables that answer the owner's four questions.
  • We check our answers against known correct totals, so we are not guessing.

Intermediate Profiling and quality rules

  • Grain and key: one row = one order, key order_id (e.g. KHR-0914-00610 = canteen, month-day, counter). Duplicates are rows with the same key.
  • Rules: unparsable line → quarantine; student_id = TEST → drop; same order_id twice → keep one; item not in menu → keep, category unknown; amount ≠ qty × menu price → keep, flag and measure the gap.
  • Business date comes from the order's timestamp, not from the file name: 2,100 orders arrive in a later file and must still count on their own day.

Advanced Design decisions you must defend

  • Deduplication needs all copies in one place: a copy can sit in the next day's file, so the dedupe key must be order_id across all files. That is a shuffle: MapReduce keyed by order_id (Module 8) does exactly this.
  • Keep the earliest copy: it tells us when the order first arrived, which decides "late".
  • Never fix money silently: Panvel's till charged old prices. Revenue stays what was actually charged; the ₹ gap is reported as a finding, not "corrected".
  • Partition silver by business date so a re-run of one day overwrites only that day (idempotency, Lesson 13.4).

Exam tip: For "Describe a big data project you did", start with the problem and the data, not the tools: "4 canteens, 130 daily files, 3.5 lakh orders with duplicates, late rows and price errors; the goal was daily revenue, item mix, peak hours and UPI reconciliation, verified against bank totals." Then the architecture, then results.

Animated example: how one till creates dirty data

A Saturday at the Kharghar till 6 steps
Animated example
  1. On a normal day the till writes one line per order into that day's file.
  2. The canteen's wifi drops; the till keeps billing and stores orders locally.
  3. When the network returns it re-sends recent orders, so order 00377 appears twice.
  4. Orders still unsent at closing go out with the next file. The canteen is shut on Sunday, so they land in Monday's file.
  5. If the till crashes while writing, a line is cut off half-way.
  6. None of this is rare. The rules handle it: one row per order_id, business date from the timestamp, broken lines to quarantine. (Illustrative; our generator plants the same problems at known rates.)

Write the rules down before you write code, and give each rule a counter. "We dropped some bad rows" is not acceptable in a review; "296 lines quarantined, here they are" is.

5 See it happen

Landing 130 files: the terminal and the NameNode

On the left what you type and see; on the right what HDFS stores and what it costs.

From pen drive to bronze 6 steps
What you see
  1. The generator writes 132 files (130 data files plus menu.csv and truth.json).
  2. land.sh creates one folder per canteen and copies its 26 daily files.
  3. hdfs dfs -count: 104 order files and 26 payment files in bronze.
  4. du shows logical size and size with 3 replicas: 7.0 MB of Kharghar data uses 20.9 MB of disk.
  5. fsck: 130 files in 130 blocks, each only about 0.4 MB.
  6. The cleaning rules, run locally, reproduce every number in truth.json.
Behind the screen
  1. Until we put them, the files exist only on the local disk; HDFS knows nothing.
  2. Each -put asks the NameNode for a block and streams it through a pipeline of DataNodes (Lesson 6.1).
  3. After landing, the NameNode holds 130 file and 130 block entries in memory.
  4. With replication 3 on 3 workers, every worker stores a copy of every block: 52 MB of data uses about 156 MB of disk.
  5. Each block is about 0.4 MB. Small files waste NameNode memory and create one map task each.
  6. The local pipe test runs the same Python on one machine: it checks the rules, not the cluster. (Block squares are symbolic.)
6 Real-world use

The same first week, in other companies

1. Retail chain: store sales

A supermarket chain receives nightly POS files from hundreds of stores. Before building dashboards, the team profiles them and finds re-sent batches, test transactions and stores with outdated price lists, exactly our dirt at a larger scale.

Store tills→inventory + profiling + rules→Trusted sales tables

2. Payments: settlement reconciliation

A payment aggregator matches merchant orders with bank and UPI switch files every day. The acceptance test is the bank statement: every rupee must be matched, refunded or explained.

Orders + bank files→match on transaction key→Exceptions report

3. Highways: toll transactions

Toll lanes upload FASTag reads in batches; lanes that lose connectivity upload hours later. Reports must use the time of passage, not upload time, the same "business date from the timestamp" rule.

Lane batches→event time, dedupe on read ID→Daily revenue per plaza

4. Hospitals: pharmacy sales

A hospital group finds medicines sold that are missing from the central drug master. Like our maggi, they are kept and flagged, and the master-data team is told, instead of silently losing revenue from reports.

Branch sales→join with master, flag unknown→Fix the master

5. Any migration

When a company moves pipelines to a new platform, the old system's output is the truth file. The new pipeline is accepted only when counts and totals match, day by day (Lesson 13.4).

Old reports→acceptance tests→Cut-over

Simplified; organisations differ in tools and policies. Kadak Canteens and its data are fictional.

1 · Generate the dataset (on your PC)

# make_capstone.py: the capstone dataset for "Kadak Canteens", 4 college canteens in Navi Mumbai (September 2026)
# Raw POS files per canteen per day, UPI gateway files per day, the menu, and truth.json
# (the right answers, so your pipeline can be checked). Same files on every computer.
import csv, json, os, random
from datetime import date, datetime, timedelta

random.seed(14)
OUT = "capstone_raw"
COLLEGES = {"KHR": 4500, "VSH": 3800, "PNL": 3000, "NRL": 2200}      # orders on a normal day
MENU = [  # item, category, price, valid_from, valid_to
    ("chai", "drink", 15, "2026-09-01", "2026-09-15"), ("chai", "drink", 18, "2026-09-16", "2026-09-30"),
    ("coffee", "drink", 20, "2026-09-01", "2026-09-30"), ("samosa", "snack", 18, "2026-09-01", "2026-09-30"),
    ("vada pav", "snack", 20, "2026-09-01", "2026-09-30"), ("poha", "breakfast", 25, "2026-09-01", "2026-09-30"),
    ("idli", "breakfast", 30, "2026-09-01", "2026-09-30"), ("dosa", "meal", 45, "2026-09-01", "2026-09-30"),
    ("thali", "meal", 80, "2026-09-01", "2026-09-30")]
WEIGHT = {"chai": 30, "coffee": 8, "samosa": 14, "vada pav": 14, "poha": 9, "idli": 9, "dosa": 10, "thali": 6}
HOURS = [(8, 14), (9, 12), (10, 7), (11, 6), (12, 9), (13, 15), (14, 9), (15, 6), (16, 10), (17, 7), (18, 5)]

def next_open(d):                                # the next day the canteen is open
    return d + timedelta(2 if d.weekday() == 5 else 1)

def price(item, day):
    for it, cat, p, f, t in MENU:
        if it == item and f <= day <= t:
            return p

os.makedirs(f"{OUT}/payments", exist_ok=True)
with open(f"{OUT}/menu.csv", "w", newline="") as f:
    csv.writer(f).writerows([("item", "category", "price", "valid_from", "valid_to")] + MENU)

files = {}                 # (college, file_day) -> list of raw lines
pays = {}                  # day -> list of gateway events
truth = {"clean_orders": 0, "revenue": 0, "duplicates": 0, "malformed": 0, "test_orders": 0,
         "late": 0, "unknown_item": 0, "price_mismatch": 0, "undercharged": 0, "by_college": {}, "upi_net": 0, "refunds": 0, "upi_unconfirmed": 0}
days = [date(2026, 9, 1) + timedelta(d) for d in range(30)]
for c, base in COLLEGES.items():
    truth["by_college"][c] = {"orders": 0, "revenue": 0}
    for d in days:
        if d.weekday() == 6:                      # canteens are closed on Sundays
            continue
        ds = d.isoformat()
        n = int(base * random.uniform(0.85, 1.15))
        for k in range(1, n + 1):
            oid = f"{c}-{d:%m%d}-{k:05d}"
            h = random.choices([h for h, w in HOURS], [w for h, w in HOURS])[0]
            ts = f"{ds} {h:02d}:{random.randint(0, 59):02d}:{random.randint(0, 59):02d}"
            item = random.choices(list(WEIGHT), list(WEIGHT.values()))[0]
            if c == "KHR" and ds >= "2026-09-21" and random.random() < 0.04:
                item = "maggi"                          # new dish, nobody added it to menu.csv
            qty = random.choices([1, 2, 3], [80, 15, 5])[0]
            p = 30 if item == "maggi" else price(item, ds)
            if c == "PNL" and item == "chai" and ds >= "2026-09-16":
                p = 15                                  # Panvel's POS never got the new chai price
            student = f"S{random.randint(1, 9000):05d}"
            if c == "VSH" and ds == "2026-09-03" and k % 250 == 0:
                student = "TEST"                        # a POS engineer testing the till
            mode = random.choices(["UPI", "CASH", "CARD"], [70, 25, 5])[0]
            row = f"{oid},{ts},{c},{student},{item},{qty},{qty * p},{mode}"
            fday = ds
            if random.random() < 0.006:                 # POS offline: row arrives in the next day's file
                fday = next_open(d).isoformat()
                if fday > "2026-09-30":
                    fday = ds
            files.setdefault((c, fday), []).append(row)
            if student == "TEST":
                truth["test_orders"] += 1
                continue
            truth["clean_orders"] += 1; truth["revenue"] += qty * p
            truth["by_college"][c]["orders"] += 1; truth["by_college"][c]["revenue"] += qty * p
            truth["late"] += fday != ds
            truth["unknown_item"] += item == "maggi"
            if item != "maggi" and p != price(item, ds):
                truth["price_mismatch"] += 1; truth["undercharged"] += qty * (price(item, ds) - p)
            if random.random() < 0.008:                 # POS re-sent the row (same or next file)
                again = fday if random.random() < 0.7 else min(next_open(date.fromisoformat(fday)).isoformat(), "2026-09-30")
                files.setdefault((c, again), []).append(row)
                truth["duplicates"] += 1
            if mode == "UPI":
                txn = f"T{random.randint(10**9, 10**10 - 1)}"
                ev = pays.setdefault(ds, [])
                outage = c == "NRL" and ds == "2026-09-22" and h == 13   # gateway down: no confirmations
                if random.random() < 0.01:              # first attempt failed, student paid again
                    ev.append({"txn_id": txn + "F", "order_id": oid, "amount": qty * p, "status": "FAILED", "ts": ts})
                if outage:
                    truth["upi_unconfirmed"] += qty * p
                else:
                    ev.append({"txn_id": txn, "order_id": oid, "amount": qty * p, "status": "SUCCESS", "ts": ts})
                    truth["upi_net"] += qty * p
                if random.random() < 0.004 and not outage:   # refunded later (cold food, wrong item)
                    ev.append({"txn_id": txn + "R", "order_id": oid, "amount": -qty * p, "status": "REFUNDED", "ts": ts})
                    truth["upi_net"] -= qty * p; truth["refunds"] += 1

bad = ["{oid},{ts},{c},S00042,dosa", "{oid},{ts},{c},S00042,dosa,1,forty five,UPI", "{oid},2026-09-31 10:00:00,{c},S00042,idli,1,30,CASH"]
for (c, ds), rows in sorted(files.items()):
    random.shuffle(rows)
    for j in range(random.randint(0, 6)):                # a few broken lines per file
        rows.insert(random.randrange(len(rows)), random.choice(bad).format(oid=f"{c}-BAD-{ds}-{j}", ts=ds + " 10:00:00", c=c))
        truth["malformed"] += 1
    os.makedirs(f"{OUT}/orders/{c}", exist_ok=True)
    with open(f"{OUT}/orders/{c}/orders_{c}_{ds}.csv", "w") as f:
        f.write("order_id,ts,college,student_id,item,qty,amount,pay_mode\n" + "\n".join(rows) + "\n")
for ds, ev in sorted(pays.items()):
    with open(f"{OUT}/payments/upi_{ds}.jsonl", "w") as f:
        f.write("".join(json.dumps(e) + "\n" for e in ev))
json.dump(truth, open(f"{OUT}/truth.json", "w"), indent=1)

size = sum(os.path.getsize(os.path.join(r, x)) for r, _, fs in os.walk(OUT) for x in fs)
nfiles = sum(len(fs) for _, _, fs in os.walk(OUT))
print(f"{nfiles} files, {size:,} bytes in {OUT}/")
print(f"clean orders {truth['clean_orders']:,}, revenue Rs {truth['revenue']:,}")
for k in ["duplicates", "malformed", "test_orders", "late", "unknown_item", "price_mismatch", "undercharged", "refunds", "upi_unconfirmed"]:
    print(f"  {k:<15}{truth[k]:>7,}")

#!/usr/bin/env python3
# clean_mapper.py: parse raw POS lines; key every good row by order_id so copies meet in one reducer
import os, sys
from datetime import datetime

path = os.environ.get("mapreduce_map_input_file", "orders_XXX_0000-00-00.csv")
fday = path.rsplit("_", 1)[-1][:10]                      # date of the file the line arrived in

def count(name):
    print(f"reporter:counter:capstone,{name},1", file=sys.stderr)

for line in sys.stdin:
    line = line.rstrip("\n")
    if not line or line.startswith("order_id,"):
        continue                                          # header of each file
    f = line.split(",")
    try:
        if len(f) != 8:
            raise ValueError("fields")
        datetime.strptime(f[1], "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")      # rejects 2026-09-31
        int(f[5]), int(f[6])
    except ValueError:
        count("MALFORMED")
        print(f"~BAD\t{fday}\t{line}")                    # quarantine, keep the raw line
        continue
    if f[3] == "TEST":
        count("TEST_ORDER")
        continue
    print(f"{f[0]}\t{fday}\t{line}")

#!/usr/bin/env python3
# clean_reducer.py: one line per order_id (keep the earliest copy), add category, flag problems
import csv, sys

menu = {}                                                 # item -> [(from, to, price, category)]
for r in csv.DictReader(open("menu.csv")):
    menu.setdefault(r["item"], []).append((r["valid_from"], r["valid_to"], int(r["price"]), r["category"]))

def count(name, k=1):
    print(f"reporter:counter:capstone,{name},{k}", file=sys.stderr)

def finish(copies):
    fday, line = min(copies)                              # earliest file wins
    count("DUPLICATE", len(copies) - 1)
    oid, ts, college, student, item, qty, amount, mode = line.split(",")
    dt, qty, amount = ts[:10], int(qty), int(amount)
    late = fday > dt
    price = next(((p, c) for a, b, p, c in menu.get(item, []) if a <= dt <= b), None)
    category = price[1] if price else "unknown"
    under = qty * price[0] - amount if price else 0
    count("CLEAN")
    if late: count("LATE")
    if not price: count("UNKNOWN_ITEM")
    if under: count("PRICE_MISMATCH"); count("UNDERCHARGED_RS", under)
    print("\t".join(map(str, ["OK", oid, dt, ts, college, student, item, category, qty, amount, mode,
                              int(late), int(not price), under])))

key, copies = None, []
for line in sys.stdin:
    k, fday, raw = line.rstrip("\n").split("\t", 2)
    if k == "~BAD":
        print(f"BAD\t{fday}\t{raw}")
        continue
    if k != key and copies:
        finish(copies); copies = []
    key = k
    copies.append((fday, raw))
if copies:
    finish(copies)

Interview and viva questions

How do you start a data engineering project?

By writing down the business questions and deliverables, listing every source (format, frequency, volume, key, owner), profiling the real data for counts, keys, nulls, invalid values, duplicates and late records, agreeing rules for each problem, and agreeing acceptance criteria against a trusted source before building the pipeline.

What is data profiling?

Measuring what the data really contains before using it: row counts per source and day, distinct and duplicate keys, null and invalid values, value ranges, unexpected categories and arrival delays. Profiling turns assumptions ("order_id is unique") into facts ("2,784 order_ids appear twice").

What is the grain of a table and why does it matter?

The grain is what one row represents, for example one order. Every aggregation, join and deduplication rule depends on it: if you think a row is an order but it is really an order line, revenue and counts are wrong.

How do you handle late-arriving data?

Use the event time (when the order happened) for business dates, not the arrival or file date; partition by event date; and make loads idempotent so a late row can be added by re-running that day without double counting. Flag late rows so delays can be monitored.

Why keep bad rows instead of deleting them?

Because someone must be able to see what was rejected and why: a quarantine table with the raw line and a reason makes the pipeline auditable, lets source teams fix their systems, and proves that the totals add up (clean + quarantined + dropped = received).

P8Advanced

Write an acceptance test

Input
two dictionaries
Expected output

PASS revenue 11524009 11524009
FAIL late 2101 2100
2/3 passed
Show solution and explanation 
truth = {"orders": 354135, "revenue": 11524009, "late": 2100}
result = {"orders": 354135, "revenue": 11524009, "late": 2101}
def check(result, truth):
    ok = 0
    for k in truth:
        good = result.get(k) == truth[k]
        ok += good
        print("PASS" if good else "FAIL", k, result.get(k), truth[k])
    print(f"{ok}/{len(truth)} passed")
check(result, truth)

Real-world scenario questions (asked in company interviews)

Interviewers love "your first week on a project" questions. These are the common ones.

Project start · processFresher

"You get a pen drive of files and a vague request. What do you do first?"

A small business wants "reports" from its till and payment files.

The interviewer is checking: that you clarify before you code.

Show a strong answer
  • Turn "reports" into concrete questions with the owner (revenue per canteen per day, item mix, peak hours, UPI reconciliation) and agree who uses them and how often.
  • Inventory the sources: format, frequency, volume, key, owner, and find a trusted total to check against (bank statement).
  • Profile, list the problems with counts, agree rules, then design the pipeline.
Data quality · judgementFresher

"One shop charged the old price for two weeks. Do you correct the revenue?"

The menu says ₹18; the till charged ₹15.

The interviewer is checking: that you don't change financial facts silently.

Show a strong answer
  • No. Revenue is what customers actually paid; it must match the cash and UPI received.
  • Flag the rows, compute the gap (₹43,710 in our data) and report it to the business with the shop and dates, so they fix the till.
  • If finance wants a "list price" view, add it as a separate column, never overwrite the amount.
Design · late dataExperienced

"Some shops upload yesterday's sales today. How do you design for that?"

Daily reports were already sent when the late rows arrive.

The interviewer is checking: event time vs arrival time, idempotency.

Show a strong answer
  • Partition by event date from the timestamp; keep the arrival file and time as lineage columns and flag late rows.
  • Re-process the affected event dates (a small backfill) with overwrite-by-partition, so re-runs never double count.
  • Measure lateness; agree a cut-off (e.g. reports final after 2 days) and re-issue changed reports.
Testing · trustExperienced

"How do you know your pipeline's numbers are right?"

The business has been burned by wrong dashboards before.

The interviewer is checking: reconciliation and automated checks.

Show a strong answer
  • Reconcile against an independent source: bank or gateway totals, source-system row counts, the old report during migration.
  • Prove conservation: clean + quarantined + dropped = received, per day and source.
  • Automate it: acceptance checks run after every load and fail the pipeline before gold is published.
